# Pokémon Omnis — Complete Main Story Bible

## Story Premise

Pokémon Omnis is an adult continuation of the Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn generation of stories. The player chooses one of six returning protagonists—Red, Leaf, Ethan, Kris, Brendan, or May—and begins in that character's home region. Years have passed since the original regional journeys. The heroes are experienced but no longer invincible, the regional Leagues have become larger institutions, and the old disasters have left political, environmental, and personal scars.

The opening should still feel like a traditional Pokémon adventure: a hometown, a professor, a partner, a new team, routes, towns, Gyms, and a regional mystery that begins as ordinary crime. The darker story emerges gradually. Team Rocket is no longer a gang loudly announcing itself in every city. Giovanni has rebuilt it as a network of security firms, shipping companies, laboratories, political donors, smugglers, and true believers. Its crimes first resemble industrial espionage, museum theft, utility sabotage, and black-market research.

Giovanni's hidden project is the **Mythic Siphon**, a machine designed to graft four mythic powers onto Mewtwo:

- **Kanto:** Mew-derived genetic residue and the surviving science of the original Mewtwo project.
- **Johto:** Celebi's ability to repair and cross time.
- **Hoenn:** Jirachi's power to convert wishes into reality.
- **Sevii:** Deoxys's ability to mutate and adapt beyond natural limits.

Giovanni does not describe the project as conquest. He argues that the world has survived too many legendary catastrophes, regional wars, criminal teams, and incompetent institutions. His answer is a single controllable guardian powerful enough to deter every government, League, and legendary Pokémon. His diagnosis contains uncomfortable truths; his solution reduces life, hope, memory, and freedom to machinery.

The player cannot prevent every loss. Rocket obtains each channel. The heroes' victories instead expose the plan, save civilians, fracture Rocket's network, and create the only conditions under which the final disaster can be reversed.

## Campaign Structure

The three regional storylines are **alternate origin campaigns**, each designed for approximately **35–40 hours** including ordinary exploration, eight home-region Gym challenges, rival battles, and major optional dialogue. The player does not complete three separate 40-hour stories in one save.

Each origin campaign follows the same large structure:

1. **Homecoming and ordinary League journey** — 3–4 hours.
2. **Grounded Rocket crime in the home region** — 4–5 hours.
3. **First foreign-region investigation** — 4–5 hours.
4. **Home-region escalation** — 7–9 hours.
5. **Second foreign-region investigation** — 4–5 hours.
6. **Home-region mythic-channel climax** — 5–6 hours.
7. **Interregional alliance and Sevii operation** — 4–5 hours.
8. **Cinnabar failure, Faraway Island, and second assault** — 5–6 hours.

The selected route changes:

- which regional mystery the player understands first;
- which professor has the closest relationship with the player;
- which protagonist is the player's primary rival and partner;
- which moral failure feels most personal;
- which foreign-region events the player witnesses directly;
- several scenes and tactical roles during the finale.

The same larger canon remains true in all routes. While the player is working in one region, the other adult protagonists are conducting their own investigations. By the midpoint, the six heroes and three professors are exchanging information. By the Sevii arc, they are operating as one alliance.

## The Six Returning Protagonists

The selected character is the playable hero. The unselected protagonist from the same region becomes the main rival and field partner. The four protagonists from the other two regions appear as major allies. Their personalities remain consistent regardless of which character is selected.

### Red

Red became famous too young and withdrew from public life after years of being treated as a symbol rather than a person. He is now an exceptional wilderness trainer and quiet crisis responder. He speaks rarely, notices everything, and distrusts institutions that ask young trainers to solve adult failures. His connection to the old Rocket conflict makes Giovanni's return personal.

### Leaf

Leaf became Professor Oak's most capable field coordinator. She is direct, curious, and comfortable challenging famous people. She believes research should serve ordinary trainers and Pokémon, not private laboratories. Her central conflict is discovering how much Oak concealed about the original Mewtwo project.

### Ethan

Ethan works as a ranger, courier, and route guide across Johto. He knows the small towns, shrines, farms, and roads better than any official. He is warm and practical, but he hides how deeply the old Rocket occupation affected his family and community. He is the alliance's best civilian-rescue leader.

### Kris

Kris is an archaeologist and field linguist who studies the Ruins of Alph, traditional Johto rituals, and the relationship between human memory and Pokémon behavior. She is skeptical of superstition but respectful of sacred places. Rocket's weaponization of her research becomes one of the story's sharpest personal betrayals.

### Brendan

Brendan is an ecological surveyor who has spent years mapping migration, weather damage, and human expansion across Hoenn. He is calm under pressure and reads environments better than people. He initially believes every crisis has a natural pattern; Jirachi forces him to accept that human longing can be as powerful and dangerous as weather.

### May

May is Birch's senior expedition leader and a formidable competitive trainer. She is energetic, decisive, and good at keeping divided groups moving. She initially treats the renewed League circuit as a chance to reclaim a life that became too administrative. Her arc is learning that leadership sometimes means refusing the clean victory everyone expects.

## Major Returning Allies

### Professor Oak

Oak is the world's most respected Pokémon authority, but his reputation hides a serious failure. He knew fragments of the original Mewtwo research survived and chose secrecy over public accountability. He feared that disclosure would inspire new cloning attempts. His silence instead allowed Giovanni to recover the work without scrutiny.

### Professor Elm

Elm's studies of Pokémon development and temporal adaptation helped establish how living systems recover after unnatural disruption. Rocket uses stolen versions of his models to calculate how much damage is required to force Celebi to appear. Elm is devastated that peaceful research became a targeting system.

### Professor Birch

Birch's environmental models connect weather, meteor activity, migration, and unusual Pokémon awakenings. Devon incorporated some of his work into energy systems. Rocket combines those models with meteor research to predict Jirachi's awakening. Birch's guilt makes him initially overprotective and secretive, repeating Oak's mistake on a smaller scale.

### Blue

Blue is now a senior Kanto League official and still one of the world's strongest trainers. He understands public authority better than the six field heroes, but he is trapped by jurisdiction, evidence standards, and political pressure. He becomes the alliance's operational commander while openly accusing the professors and League of allowing Rocket to return.

### Silver

Silver has spent years dismantling Rocket remnants and refusing Giovanni's name. He is not gentle, but he is no longer cruel. His knowledge of Rocket habits repeatedly saves lives. He fears Giovanni rebuilt Rocket partly to prove that Silver's rejection changed nothing.

### Wally

Wally is now a respected Hoenn trainer and community advocate. His childhood wish was simply to become strong enough to live freely. He understands the difference between a wish that opens a life and a wish that tries to control one. Jirachi trusts him because, when finally given the chance to ask for anything, he asks for nothing for himself.

### Other Important Figures

- **Brock:** museum authority, public safety organizer, and first person to treat the Pewter theft as more than property crime.
- **Misty:** investigates water contamination and covert shipping around Cerulean.
- **Lt. Surge:** recognizes Rocket's power theft as preparation for military-scale equipment.
- **Erika:** exposes Rocket's use of legitimate pharmaceutical and agricultural fronts.
- **Sabrina:** experiences psychic echoes from Mewtwo and becomes essential to locating Cinnabar's underground core.
- **Koga and Janine:** uncover Rocket couriers using Fuchsia's protected routes.
- **Blaine:** surviving witness to the original Cinnabar project and one of the story's most morally compromised allies.
- **Mr. Fuji:** former project scientist who provides the emotional truth of Mewtwo's creation.
- **Falkner, Bugsy, Whitney, Morty, Chuck, Jasmine, Pryce, and Clair:** local leaders who protect Johto's towns as time anomalies worsen.
- **Kurt:** creates the only capture device capable of disrupting Rocket's Apricorn lattice.
- **The Kimono Girls:** guardians of oral history who understand that Celebi appears to heal injury, not answer summons.
- **Roxanne, Brawly, Flannery, Norman, Winona, Tate, Liza, Juan, and Steven:** regional leaders who connect the Hoenn investigation to geology, shipping, weather, space research, and public defense.
- **Captain Stern:** traces Rocket's disguised cargo network.
- **Wattson:** retired from the Gym but still Hoenn's foremost authority on power infrastructure.
- **Professor Cozmo:** identifies the meteor resonance linked to Jirachi.
- **Maxie and Archie:** former extremist leaders now working under strict oversight on climate restoration. They recognize Giovanni's rhetoric because they once used similar logic.
- **Bill and Celio:** expose the communications architecture linking all regions to Sevii.
- **Lorelei:** leads island defense and refuses to let mainland authorities treat Sevii as expendable territory.

## Team Rocket

### Giovanni

Giovanni has aged, but not softened. He believes history vindicated him: legendary Pokémon nearly destroyed regions, criminal teams repeatedly exploited ancient powers, and the Leagues repeatedly relied on exceptional children to avert catastrophe. He presents the Mythic Siphon as an end to uncertainty.

His deepest error is not that he underestimates Mewtwo's strength. It is that he cannot imagine a powerful being choosing anything except domination or obedience. When Mewtwo rejects him, Giovanni's entire philosophy collapses in a single moment.

### Archer

Archer rebuilt Rocket's legal and financial infrastructure. He is disciplined, ideological, and convinced Giovanni's order is preferable to democratic weakness. He commands Sevii logistics and the final occupation of Cinnabar.

### Ariana

Ariana controls public fronts, recruitment, and political influence. She offers jobs, security, and medicine in neglected communities, then makes those communities dependent on Rocket. She is the story's clearest example of Rocket functioning as an institution rather than a gang.

### Proton

Proton directs violent field operations. He deliberately creates panic and ecological damage because frightened institutions make mistakes. He leads the Sprout Tower raid, several power attacks, and the operation that wounds Ilex Forest.

### Petrel

Petrel specializes in false identities, forged companies, and misinformation. He appears under several harmless roles before being exposed. His shipping records become the first solid route to Sevii. He ultimately provides limited information when he realizes empowered Mewtwo will kill Rocket personnel as readily as anyone else.

### Dr. Mara Voss

Voss is the principal scientist of the Mythic Siphon and a former Silph contractor who later worked with Devon subsidiaries. She does not worship Giovanni. She believes consciousness can be modeled, constrained, and improved. She treats Mewtwo, Celebi, Jirachi, and Deoxys as systems whose useful properties can be separated from their identities. Her calm language makes her more disturbing than Rocket's openly violent members.

## Mystery and Tone Rules

1. **Rocket's purpose remains unclear for the first third.** Early crimes involve archives, power, cargo, radio relays, and research equipment.
2. **Each clue should have a plausible non-mythic interpretation.** The player may suspect weapons, cloning, or regional sabotage before understanding the four channels.
3. **The professors are useful but fallible.** Their secrecy, professional pride, and old compromises help create the crisis.
4. **The player wins local battles but often loses strategic objectives.** Rocket escapes with data or equipment even when grunts are defeated.
5. **The darker tone comes from consequences, not gore.** Communities lose power, people remember contradictory lives, Pokémon are injured by experiments, and trusted institutions hide mistakes.
6. **Giovanni's argument should be coherent enough to disturb the heroes.** He is wrong because he denies consent and freedom, not because every observation he makes is false.
7. **Mythic Pokémon remain persons, not prizes.** Mew, Celebi, Jirachi, and Deoxys are never ordinary checklist captures during the main story.
8. **Legendary Pokémon outside the central four appear as distant omens, guardians, or postgame threads.** They do not become a procession of mandatory boss fights.

## Shared Early Background

Before the game begins, Rocket has already spent several years rebuilding through shell companies. The organization has recovered partial Cinnabar records, purchased abandoned Silph patents, infiltrated Devon suppliers, restored Johto radio sites, and converted Sevii warehouses into black laboratories.

Oak, Elm, and Birch have each noticed separate irregularities but have not compared them fully. Oak sees requests for obsolete genetic papers. Elm sees anonymous downloads of temporal adaptation studies. Birch sees meteorological models being purchased through obscure contractors. Each assumes the problem belongs to someone else's field.

The selected protagonist returns home for a personal reason rather than because the world has selected a hero. The local professor offers a legitimate field assignment tied to a renewed League circuit. Badges function as current travel credentials, allowing access to protected routes, emergency transport, and research zones. This preserves the familiar Gym journey while making it plausible for experienced adults to begin again with a new team.


# Regional Campaign I — Kanto Origin

## Core Theme

Kanto is a story about buried responsibility. The region rebuilt over the ruins of Team Rocket and the Cinnabar disaster without ever publicly confronting what happened. Its institutions became prosperous, but much of that prosperity rests on technology derived from the same culture that created Mewtwo. Giovanni's return forces Kanto to face the difference between surviving a crime and accounting for it.

The Kanto campaign is designed for approximately **37 hours**.

## Primary Cast

- **Player:** Red or Leaf.
- **Primary rival/partner:** the unselected Kanto protagonist.
- **Regional professor:** Oak.
- **Recurring Kanto allies:** Blue, Brock, Misty, Lt. Surge, Erika, Sabrina, Koga, Janine, Blaine, Mr. Fuji, Bill.
- **First foreign-region allies:** Ethan, Kris, Elm, Silver.
- **Second foreign-region allies:** Brendan, May, Birch, Wally, Steven, Wattson.

## Act I — The Region That Moved On

### Chapter 1: Return to Pallet — 1.5 hours

The player returns to Pallet Town after years away from active League competition. Red has been living quietly and taking isolated field work; Leaf has been coordinating research expeditions but has stopped training seriously. Whichever character is selected has recently suffered a professional failure that made returning home feel safer than moving forward. The exact details remain lightly stated so the player can project onto the character.

Oak asks the player to participate in the **Kanto Recovery Survey**, a long-term study of how Pokémon populations have changed since the reconstruction of Cinnabar, the closure of old Rocket facilities, and the expansion of Saffron and Celadon. The assignment begins simply: take a sealed ecological sample and a set of survey records to Brock at the Pewter Museum.

The unselected Kanto protagonist has already agreed to the same survey and treats the player's return as both welcome and challenge. Their rivalry is adult rather than hostile. They remember how capable the player used to be and will not allow nostalgia to substitute for present ability.

Oak provides a new starter because the survey prohibits using established high-level teams in fragile habitats. The rule also serves a character purpose: the player is not reclaiming an old legend but building a new relationship from the beginning.

The first routes are deliberately ordinary. Trainers discuss work, family, changing towns, and why they returned to the League circuit. Viridian Forest contains signs of recent unauthorized equipment, but nothing clearly criminal.

### Chapter 2: The Pewter Theft — 2.5 hours

The player reaches Pewter during a public exhibition on fossil restoration. Brock receives Oak's materials and explains that the Museum is comparing modern restoration methods with archived Cinnabar techniques.

During the exhibition, power fails for forty seconds. Masked thieves breach a sealed archive and escape with three apparently unrelated items:

1. fossil regeneration calibration data;
2. a Moon Stone isotope sample from Mt. Moon;
3. an obsolete Cinnabar accession ledger listing destroyed research specimens.

The player battles a thief in the museum service tunnel and wins, but Proton detonates a nonlethal structural charge that forces Brock to choose between pursuit and protecting visitors. Rocket escapes.

The criminals do not publicly identify themselves. One defeated grunt carries a modern black uniform beneath ordinary work clothes, but Brock refuses to announce Team Rocket's return without evidence. Blue arrives as a League official and orders the building sealed. He is irritated that Oak's delivery coincided with the theft and immediately suspects someone knew the archive would be opened.

The player earns Brock's badge after helping stabilize the museum and completing the local challenge. The badge is not a reward for heroism; Brock insists the player prove they can still make sound decisions under pressure.

### Chapter 3: Moonlight Inventory — 2 hours

Brock asks the player and rival to verify whether the stolen Moon Stone sample was the real target. In Mt. Moon they find abandoned drilling equipment, sedated wild Pokémon, and evidence that the thieves tested several mineral deposits before the museum robbery.

Misty joins after Cerulean's water authority discovers trace compounds associated with psychic suppressants in runoff from an old tunnel. The player follows the tunnel to a temporary laboratory. The lab contains no legendary imagery—only tissue-growth chambers, fossil reconstruction notes, and power-consumption estimates.

A recorded message from Dr. Voss refers to a **viable vessel** and a **genetic anchor**. The terms sound like cloning, but no species is named.

The rival argues that Oak is withholding context. Oak admits the stolen ledger referenced research transferred from Cinnabar before the island's destruction, but claims he never saw its full contents. This is the first crack in the player's trust.

After the Cerulean Gym challenge, Misty provides shipping logs showing that the lab's chemicals entered Kanto through a company registered in Goldenrod City.

## Act II — The Goldenrod Connection

### Chapter 4: Across the Magnet Line — 4 hours

Blue cannot authorize a Kanto raid in Johto based on circumstantial evidence, but Oak contacts Elm and arranges a research exchange. The player and rival travel together by Magnet Train, preserving the feeling that they are partners rather than a hero with a follower.

Goldenrod initially feels safe. Ethan and Kris meet them at the station. One is investigating stolen radio components; the other has traced forged archaeological permits connected to the same shell company. Silver appears independently and openly accuses Blue and Oak of reacting years too late.

The investigation moves through three layers:

- **Department Store:** Rocket purchased industrial batteries and medical restraints through intermediaries.
- **Radio Tower:** obsolete broadcast equipment was removed from storage under forged League orders.
- **Underground:** a relay station briefly transmits a pattern that causes nearby clocks to lose eleven seconds.

The player helps Ethan and Kris raid the relay. Petrel, disguised as a telecommunications inspector, escapes after revealing that the Kanto theft is part of a project larger than cloning. The heroes recover a signal map connecting Goldenrod to Saffron, New Mauville, and the Sevii Islands.

Elm examines the signal and notes that it resembles biological recovery cycles rather than ordinary encryption. Kris recognizes fragments of Unown spacing inside the transmission. Neither can explain why a Kanto genetic operation would need temporal data.

During the Johto chapter, the Kanto player can challenge a special interregional exhibition rather than Johto's full Gym circuit. Ethan and Kris battle the player and rival as a four-person test. The scene establishes mutual respect and gives all four protagonists distinct battle styles.

Before leaving, Silver privately tells the player that Giovanni is alive. Silver has no proof he can share, only a pattern of orders and punishments that no Rocket successor would imitate so precisely. The player does not fully believe him.

## Act III — Ghosts in Kanto

### Chapter 5: Lavender's Confession — 3.5 hours

Back in Kanto, the signal map points to Lavender Town. Rocket has been buying properties through a hospice charity and removing old medical archives from the Pokémon House.

Mr. Fuji initially denies knowing why. When a group of rescued Pokémon reacts violently to a recovered psychic restraint, he confesses that he worked on the original Mewtwo project. Blaine was not the only surviving scientist, and the project was not one clean experiment. Researchers repeatedly produced unstable organisms, discarded failed tissue, and used living Pokémon to test psychic tolerance.

Fuji explains that the stolen Cinnabar ledger identifies a preserved **Mew-derived culture**—not Mew itself, but living genetic residue used to stabilize Mewtwo's genome. The culture was believed destroyed. Oak admits he learned of it years later and chose not to disclose it publicly. He feared another cloning race and believed silence was containment.

The emotional center of the chapter is not exposition but a visit to a sealed lower room beneath the Pokémon House. Several elderly Pokémon still suffer behavioral effects from early restraint experiments. Fuji has cared for them for years. The rival is furious that the respected adults transformed guilt into private penance while leaving the evidence vulnerable.

Rocket attacks the facility to seize Fuji. The player protects the residents while the rival pursues Ariana. Ariana escapes but leaves behind proof that the charity front has been moving equipment through Celadon.

The player completes the Celadon Gym challenge after working with Erika to expose a pharmaceutical greenhouse producing neural suppressants. Erika emphasizes that useful medicine and coercive drugs can come from the same plant; intent and oversight matter.

### Chapter 6: The Power to Hold a God — 3.5 hours

Lt. Surge reports synchronized power thefts around the Kanto Power Plant. Each loss is too brief to cause a public outage, but the accumulated energy is enormous. The thieves are charging portable storage cells over weeks to avoid detection.

The player, rival, Surge, and Misty enter maintenance tunnels beneath the plant. Rocket has installed a prototype psychic-damping field built from stolen Silph patents. Wild Electric Pokémon are being used as living regulators and collapse when the field spikes.

The player defeats Proton, but Voss remotely overloads the system and transfers the stored energy away. Surge recognizes the design as a prison power supply, not a weapon. Whatever Rocket is building must remain contained even while producing catastrophic psychic force.

A brief psychic shock passes across Kanto. Sabrina contacts Blue before anyone can reach her: she heard a voice beneath Cinnabar say, **“I remember the cage.”**

The player challenges Surge after the crisis. He does not romanticize the fight. He says badges are proof that a trainer can command power without becoming dependent on it.

### Chapter 7: Silph's Clean Hands — 4 hours

Saffron is the midpoint of the Kanto route. Blue calls Oak, the player, the rival, Sabrina, and Silph leadership to compare evidence. Silph executives insist all relevant patents were decommissioned. Leaf or Red discovers that several were sold legally to a medical technology firm later acquired by one of Ariana's fronts.

Rocket then stages a hostage crisis inside an abandoned Silph annex. The attack appears to be an attempt to steal a Master Ball prototype, deliberately inviting everyone to interpret the plot through old history. The actual objective is hidden below the annex: a control architecture designed to translate human commands into psychic compulsions.

The player and rival split paths. One rescues staff with Blue; the other follows Sabrina into the lower network. Sabrina encounters a psychic imprint of Mewtwo's original captivity and nearly loses control. The player must protect her physically while she guides them mentally.

At the core, Dr. Voss reveals herself. She argues that no creature born as a weapon can meaningfully consent because its preferences were produced by human design. Therefore, controlling Mewtwo is merely completing the work that created it. Sabrina rejects this: origin does not erase personhood.

Voss escapes with the control architecture. The heroes recover a fragment naming the project **OMNIS**, divided into four channels labeled Genesis, Continuity, Desire, and Adaptation.

Oak identifies Genesis as Kanto's genetic channel. Elm remotely identifies Continuity as temporal. Birch warns that Desire may correspond to unusual energy research stolen from Devon.

The player completes the Saffron Gym challenge. Sabrina's badge marks the moment the investigation ceases to be regional.

## Act IV — The Hoenn Power Trail

### Chapter 8: New Mauville — 4 hours

Birch requests immediate help in Hoenn. Devon has suffered a theft, and the stolen focusing core produces the same energy profile as Kanto's portable cells. The player and rival travel to Slateport, where Brendan and May meet them alongside Captain Stern.

The Kanto pair sees Hoenn's investigation from an outsider's perspective. Brendan is tracking environmental effects; May is tracing people and cargo. Their methods initially clash with the Kanto pair's focus on criminal infrastructure.

The group follows a false shipping company from Slateport to Mauville. Wattson explains that tiny power fluctuations have occurred for months. He sends the six-person team—player, rival, Brendan, May, plus rotating support from Wally and Stern—into New Mauville.

Inside, they discover Rocket has been testing **resonance lenses**: devices that make one energy source behave like another. The stolen Devon core could allow genetic, temporal, or psychic energy to be converted into a common format.

Ariana attempts to collapse part of the station. Wally remains behind to evacuate maintenance workers while the protagonists pursue her. The player must choose rescue over capture, reinforcing the campaign's recurring lesson. Ariana escapes with the final calibration data.

Birch shows the alliance meteor readings that spike whenever the lenses operate. Professor Cozmo believes the signal resembles old accounts of Jirachi, but the idea remains uncertain.

Before the Kanto pair leaves Hoenn, the four protagonists present share a quiet scene in Verdanturf. They discuss why each stopped living like the child the public remembers. The scene turns the crossover cast into people rather than cameos.

## Act V — Kanto Occupied From Within

### Chapter 9: Fuchsia Routes — 3 hours

Returning to Kanto, the player follows Petrel's shipping map through Fuchsia. Koga and Janine reveal that Rocket couriers have used protected Safari routes because League inspectors avoid disturbing habitats.

The player uncovers a transfer point containing cloned tissue that reacts to nearby Pokémon with fear and aggression. Mr. Fuji confirms it is derived from the original Mew culture. It is alive, but it is not a conscious Mew.

Petrel appears disguised as a ranger. The rival recognizes a verbal habit from Goldenrod and exposes him. Under pressure, Petrel admits Cinnabar is already occupied below ground. He refuses to reveal the entrance but warns that Giovanni is not trying to create another Mewtwo; **he already has one**.

Petrel escapes during a Rocket extraction, but he deliberately leaves a Sevii routing cipher. It is unclear whether this is a mistake or the beginning of his betrayal.

The player completes the Fuchsia Gym challenge and receives Koga's authorization to enter restricted Seafoam passages.

### Chapter 10: The Ash Laboratory — 4.5 hours

Blaine has operated a temporary Gym and research station near Seafoam since Cinnabar's destruction. He initially refuses to return to the island. When shown the living culture, he admits he designed the first stabilization process that made Mewtwo viable.

Blaine explains the final Kanto truth: Mewtwo's genome contains a Mew-derived **genetic anchor** that prevents its psychic power from tearing its body apart. The stolen culture can reactivate and expand that anchor, making Mewtwo capable of accepting foreign powers without immediate collapse.

The player, rival, Blaine, Blue, and Fuji enter Cinnabar through flooded service tunnels. Above them, the island appears abandoned. Below, Rocket has restored laboratories through the old Mansion foundations.

The chapter alternates between exploration of old ruins and an active Rocket facility. Audio records reveal scientists discussing Mewtwo as inventory while other recordings show younger Blaine and Fuji arguing that ending the project would make their previous victims meaningless. Their rationalization is painfully human: they continued because admitting failure felt worse than causing more harm.

The group reaches the preserved culture. Giovanni appears in person for the first time. He thanks Oak indirectly for protecting the secret. He says institutions always hide dangerous knowledge, and hidden knowledge belongs to whoever is willing to retrieve it.

The player defeats Giovanni's field team, but the battle is a delay. Voss transfers the culture into a sealed vessel and activates the Genesis channel. The entire facility shakes with a distant psychic response.

Mew briefly appears in the ruined upper Mansion. It does not attack. It frees laboratory Pokémon, looks at the player, and vanishes toward the sea. Giovanni watches the security feed with something close to reverence but makes no attempt to capture it. He already possesses what he needs.

Rocket evacuates the Genesis vessel by submarine toward Sevii. The heroes save Blaine and Fuji from the collapsing lab but cannot stop the transfer.

After the evacuation, Blaine conducts his temporary Seafoam Gym challenge. He frames it as a test of whether the player can continue making disciplined choices after learning that respected mentors caused profound harm. This supplies Kanto's seventh badge before the Viridian certification.

### Chapter 11: Viridian Reckoning — 2.5 hours

Viridian becomes the political conclusion of the Kanto branch. Blue presents evidence to the League and demands emergency interregional authority. Several officials resist, fearing panic and economic damage. The player and rival expose an Ariana-controlled security contractor embedded in the city's emergency network.

The final Kanto Gym challenge occurs against Blue or his appointed successor. It is framed as certification for the coming operation, not as a ceremonial ending. Blue tests whether the player can adapt without knowing the opponent's strategy—the exact skill required against Rocket.

After the battle, Oak, Elm, and Birch hold their first full conference. Red, Leaf, Ethan, Kris, Brendan, and May attend in person or by secure link. The evidence establishes:

- Kanto supplied the biological vessel and genetic anchor.
- Johto is being prepared as a temporal trap.
- Hoenn's stolen technology can convert mythic energy into a usable channel.
- Sevii is the logistics center connecting all three operations.

Before the alliance can act, Ethan and Kris report a catastrophic anomaly forming around Ilex Forest. The Kanto player and rival travel briefly to Johto in time to help evacuate civilians, but Celebi's capture is primarily led by the Johto heroes in this route. The player witnesses Rocket's temporal cage close from outside and sees Celebi taken.

Soon after, Brendan and May report Jirachi's capture in Hoenn. The Kanto route does not replay those full regional climaxes, but the losses occur on screen through short playable support sequences and communications. All three channels are now in motion.

The routing cipher Petrel left points to One Island.

## Kanto Route Character Resolution

The Kanto protagonist begins by trying to return to an earlier, simpler identity. The route ends with the realization that there is no clean return. The old victory over Rocket did not permanently solve the region's failures, and fame did not make the hero responsible for every future crime. The adult choice is neither withdrawal nor solitary heroism; it is building an alliance that can survive without one legendary trainer carrying everything.

Oak admits his secrecy helped Giovanni. He does not receive immediate forgiveness. The rival tells him that confession is the beginning of responsibility, not the end.


# Regional Campaign II — Johto Origin

## Core Theme

Johto is a story about memory, tradition, and the violence of forcing the past to serve the present. Rocket does not summon Celebi with a ritual. It deliberately damages time until Celebi has no moral choice but to intervene. The region's sacred places become frightening because familiar details begin behaving incorrectly.

The Johto campaign is designed for approximately **38 hours**.

## Primary Cast

- **Player:** Ethan or Kris.
- **Primary rival/partner:** the unselected Johto protagonist.
- **Regional professor:** Elm.
- **Recurring Johto allies:** Silver, Falkner, Bugsy, Whitney, Morty, Chuck, Jasmine, Pryce, Clair, Kurt, the Kimono Girls.
- **First foreign-region allies:** Red, Leaf, Oak, Blue, Sabrina, Mr. Fuji.
- **Second foreign-region allies:** Brendan, May, Birch, Wally, Steven, Professor Cozmo.

## Act I — Ordinary Seasons

### Chapter 1: New Bark Again — 1.5 hours

The player returns to New Bark after years away. Ethan has spent much of adulthood working routes and ranger contracts; Kris has spent years in ruins and archives. The selected hero has become competent at work but disconnected from the companionship that made the original journey meaningful.

Elm asks the player to test a new field method for studying how Pokémon adapt to seasonal change. The work requires traveling through Johto and renewing League credentials. The first task is harmless: deliver a packet of growth records to Elder Li at Sprout Tower and compare the tower's long-kept plant observations with Elm's modern data.

The unselected protagonist is already assisting Elm and becomes the player's rival. Their relationship carries affection and unresolved competition. Silver appears briefly outside the lab but refuses to explain why he is asking whether old Rocket frequencies have been detected.

### Chapter 2: The Sprout Tower Raid — 2.5 hours

Violet City is preparing a seasonal ceremony. Falkner treats the returning heroes as respected adults, not chosen children. During the ceremony, men dressed as maintenance workers seal the lower tower and steal:

1. handwritten records of unusual plant recovery;
2. bell schedules maintained for more than a century;
3. sketches showing how wild Pokémon behaved after several historical fires and earthquakes.

The player defeats Proton in an upper chamber, but he cuts a supporting cable and forces the player to save an elder and several Bellsprout rather than continue pursuit. Rocket escapes through a service route.

The crime initially looks like theft of historical agricultural data. Elder Li says the stolen records include descriptions of mornings when damaged plants appeared healthy before anyone tended them. He regards the accounts as spiritual. Elm assumes they describe rare regenerative behavior.

That night, the player hears a bell repeat a sequence no one rang. A child in Violet remembers seeing the player arrive the previous day, though the player has only just arrived.

The player challenges Falkner, who warns that observation is not the same as understanding. The first badge begins the regional journey.

### Chapter 3: Slowpoke Hours — 3 hours

In Azalea, Slowpoke disappear from the Well. The situation resembles Rocket's old cruelty, leading the heroes to expect a simple black-market operation. Kurt and Bugsy discover something stranger: each missing Slowpoke returned hours later with no physical injury, but several react to events moments before they occur.

Inside the Well, Rocket has installed devices that expose Pokémon to tiny loops of repeated sound and light. The purpose is to measure when living creatures begin anticipating repetition. Dr. Voss's notes call this **temporal stress tolerance**.

Kurt identifies fragments of specialized Apricorn resin in the machinery. Rocket has been commissioning capture shells through fake collectors. The shells are not Poké Balls; they are components of a larger lattice designed to close around something moving unpredictably.

The player wins the local battle and frees the Slowpoke. Proton escapes with the completed measurements. Silver appears and reveals that the operation used an old Rocket authentication phrase known only to Giovanni's direct command. He believes Giovanni has returned.

The rival thinks Silver may be allowing fear of his father to shape the evidence. The tension between them continues through the route.

After the Bugsy challenge, Elm calls. Anonymous users have downloaded his research on how Pokémon recover after spatial and biological disruption. The data taken from Sprout Tower could be used to predict a recovery response.

## Act II — Kanto's Psychic Echo

### Chapter 4: Saffron Afterimage — 4.5 hours

A relay recovered in Slowpoke Well contains components manufactured in Saffron. Elm contacts Oak, and the player and rival travel to Kanto by Magnet Train. Red and Leaf meet them with Blue, who dislikes Johto investigators arriving without formal jurisdiction but needs their expertise.

The crossover begins in Saffron. Sabrina has experienced visions of the same corridor at different ages: pristine, burned, flooded, and newly built. She cannot tell whether she is seeing possible futures or memories imposed from elsewhere.

The four protagonists investigate an abandoned Silph annex where Rocket has tested psychic signals against clocks, plants, and captive Pokémon. Red focuses on tactical threats, Leaf on records, Ethan on frightened workers, and Kris on repeated symbols. The four approaches establish how the alliance will eventually function.

Mr. Fuji identifies the psychic equipment as derived from Mewtwo containment research. Oak reluctantly explains that Mewtwo's psychic field sometimes caused witnesses to remember events in the wrong order. Rocket may be combining psychic perception with actual temporal instability.

A lower chamber contains Unown arranged around a radio transmitter. They have not been physically restrained; Rocket manipulates the symbols they form by changing light and sound. Kris realizes the broadcasts are not language. They are coordinates expressed as sequences of absence—moments when expected events do not occur.

Petrel impersonates a Silph archivist and nearly convinces Blue that Kris planted evidence to protect Johto. The player exposes him by comparing his account with a time-stamped memory that only makes sense if he entered the room before the official search began.

Petrel escapes. The heroes recover a chart labeled **Continuity Threshold**, showing rising levels of damage and a predicted intervention point.

Sabrina explains the implication: Rocket is not searching for Celebi. It is calculating how badly time must be wounded before Celebi comes to repair it.

Before returning to Johto, the player visits Lavender. Mr. Fuji tells them that a guardian's compassion can be exploited more easily than its weakness. The line becomes the moral center of the route.

## Act III — Johto Becomes Incorrect

### Chapter 5: Goldenrod, One Minute Missing — 3.5 hours

Goldenrod suffers a citywide anomaly. Every clock skips the same minute, but people disagree about what happened during it. Some remember darkness. Some remember hearing Giovanni's voice. Several remember nothing except a strong feeling that someone was standing behind them.

Whitney organizes civilian shelters while Ethan and Kris investigate the Radio Tower and Underground. Rocket has embedded relay equipment in legitimate maintenance upgrades over several years. Ariana used charity grants and public-safety contracts to place the devices without triggering suspicion.

The player and rival discover that the network can synchronize short temporal disruptions across large areas. A Rocket technician claims the missing minute was only a calibration. The real operation will require several sacred sites acting as anchors.

The player defeats Ariana's team but cannot prevent the transmission. In another part of the city, a pedestrian is injured after remembering a traffic signal changing before it actually changes. The incident demonstrates that the anomalies are no longer harmless atmosphere.

Whitney's Gym battle occurs after the crisis center stabilizes. Her public confidence contrasts with private fear; she admits leaders often perform certainty because citizens need somewhere to stand.

### Chapter 6: Ecruteak Remembers Two Fires — 4 hours

In Ecruteak, the Burned Tower begins appearing briefly restored at sunset. Residents remember two versions of its destruction. Morty sees figures moving inside rooms that no longer exist. The Kimono Girls close the dance theater after hearing songs finish before they begin.

The player, rival, Morty, and Silver investigate. Deep beneath the tower, Rocket has installed an anchor made from radio coils, Apricorn resin, and fragments stolen from the Ruins of Alph. The machine forces the location to alternate between historical states.

Silver confronts Proton, who taunts him with the claim that Giovanni understands Johto better than its guardians do: sacred places are predictable because people repeat the same rituals. Silver nearly abandons the mission to pursue him. The player can challenge Silver and force him to choose the anchor over revenge.

When the anchor is destroyed, the tower stabilizes, but one elderly resident permanently remembers a spouse who died in one version of the past and survived in another. The game does not resolve which memory is true. This is the first irreversible human cost.

The Kimono Girls explain that Celebi is not a ruler of time. It is more like a healer moving along damaged living history. It appears where injury threatens to spread.

After Morty's Gym challenge, the player receives a small braided paper charm used in an old ceremony. It is not magical, but its shape later helps Kris recognize the structural logic of Hoenn's Wish Tags.

### Chapter 7: The Ruins Speak Back — 3.5 hours

At the Ruins of Alph, Unown patterns begin changing while researchers observe them. Kris's earlier published translations are found inside Rocket equipment. If Kris is the player, this becomes direct guilt; if Ethan is the player, he watches his partner realize her work has been weaponized.

The patterns depict four concepts rather than four Pokémon: **birth**, **continuance**, **desire**, and **change**. Elm links them to the OMNIS channels reported by Oak and Birch.

Rocket activates a partial temporal wound beneath the ruins. For several minutes, the player explores overlapping versions of the same chambers: ancient construction, modern excavation, and a future in which the ruins have collapsed. Wild Pokémon appear frightened rather than aggressive.

The player reaches Voss, who explains that time is not sacred merely because humans cannot manufacture it. To her, Celebi's intervention is a repeatable biological response. She calls the planned injury a stimulus.

The heroes destroy the anchor before it reaches threshold. Voss escapes but leaves with final Unown calibration data. Elm realizes his recovery models supplied the missing variable.

Elm publicly admits his research was compromised. Unlike Oak, he chooses disclosure immediately, causing political anger but allowing towns to prepare. This decision saves lives later.

The Johto Gym journey continues through Cianwood and Olivine. The player completes Chuck's challenge while helping train emergency responders, then earns Jasmine's badge after securing maritime routes that may fail if temporal disturbances disrupt navigation.

## Act IV — Hoenn's Star Clock

### Chapter 8: Meteor Falls — 4.5 hours

Birch reports that a meteor shower over Hoenn produced the same missing-time signature recorded in Goldenrod. The player and rival travel to Hoenn, meeting Brendan and May in Fallarbor. Professor Cozmo has found meteor fragments whose radiation pulses at intervals matching the Unown sequence.

The four protagonists investigate Meteor Falls and a Rocket observatory hidden behind a legitimate mining claim. Brendan reads disturbed Pokémon migration; May reconstructs the expedition route; Ethan handles stranded hikers; Kris deciphers a chamber inscription.

The inscription describes a star that sleeps through generations and awakens when longing becomes loud enough. Cozmo identifies the legend as Jirachi. Birch cautions that folklore is being interpreted too quickly, but Wally notes that the Rocket equipment is measuring emotional and psychic activity around populated areas.

Rocket has discovered that temporal instability creates powerful collective wishes: people want lost minutes restored, dead loved ones returned, and uncertain futures corrected. The Johto operation is therefore doing two jobs. It will force Celebi to appear and generate the kind of human desire needed to calibrate the Jirachi channel.

At Mossdeep Space Center, Tate and Liza help compare psychic signals. They describe Jirachi and Celebi as opposite movements: one carries desire toward possibility; the other carries damage toward repair.

Ariana attacks the data center. The player and rival work with Brendan and May to protect scientists while Wally confronts a Rocket device that offers to amplify a private wish. He shuts it down without revealing what he saw.

The heroes recover evidence that Rocket will create the final Johto wound along a line connecting the Radio Tower, Lake of Rage, Ecruteak, the Ruins of Alph, and Ilex Forest.

Before leaving Hoenn, Kris and Wally compare the Johto paper charm with decorative tags used during a Mossdeep star festival. Cozmo explains an old belief: a wish written for another person is harder to corrupt than a wish for oneself. This introduces the **Wish Tag** concept well before the finale.

## Act V — The Wound in Time

### Chapter 9: Red Water, White Noise — 3.5 hours

The player returns to Johto as the Lake of Rage turns unnaturally still. Pryce reports that Pokémon are repeating the same movements. The Rocket base beneath Mahogany has been rebuilt as a synchronization hub.

The player, rival, Silver, and Pryce infiltrate the facility. They discover Rocket has stored thousands of short recordings taken from across Johto: bells, train brakes, radio announcements, cries, footsteps, and spoken names. The system uses familiar repetition to force the region into a predictable rhythm.

Proton triggers a localized loop that traps several grunts alongside civilians. Some Rocket members panic when they realize the machine does not distinguish allies. Ethan leads an evacuation; Kris uses Unown timing to interrupt the loop. Silver saves a young grunt who joined Rocket for money, demonstrating how far he has moved beyond the organization.

The hub is damaged but not destroyed. Proton escapes with the master timing key.

The Pryce challenge follows. He tells the player that endurance is not passive; surviving winter requires preparation before the cold arrives.

### Chapter 10: Blackthorn's Decision — 2.5 hours

Clair initially refuses to allow the alliance to use Dragon routes for evacuation. She fears mass movement will panic the region and disrupt sacred habitats. The player must prove the scale of the threat and complete the Blackthorn challenge.

Inside the Dragon's Den, an elder explains that traditions exist to preserve wisdom, but tradition can become vanity when leaders value appearing unshaken over protecting people. Clair changes course and opens aerial evacuation lanes.

Oak, Birch, Red, Leaf, Brendan, and May arrive in Johto. For the first time, all six protagonists and all three professors occupy the same command room. Blue coordinates League resources; Silver provides Rocket intelligence; the Kimono Girls identify likely repair sites.

The group realizes they cannot protect every anchor. Rocket designed the network so defenders must divide. The player and rival choose Ilex Forest because Celebi is most likely to appear there.

### Chapter 11: Ilex Forest Has No Tomorrow — 5 hours

Rocket activates the network. Johto does not explode; it becomes subtly impossible.

- The Magnet Train arrives before departing.
- A burned field becomes green, then burned again.
- People receive calls from their own numbers.
- Pokémon Eggs briefly show the species they might have become under different care.
- Several NPCs remember choices the player never made.

The player travels from Goldenrod to Ilex while allied teams fight to sever anchors:

- Red and Leaf hold the Radio Tower.
- Brendan and May stabilize power flow near the Lake of Rage.
- Oak and Elm work at the Ruins of Alph.
- Birch, Morty, and the Kimono Girls protect Ecruteak.
- Blue and Clair coordinate evacuation.
- Silver hunts Proton but ultimately abandons the chase to save Kurt.

In Ilex Forest, trees age and regrow within seconds. Rocket has built the **Continuity Cage**, a dome of black Apricorn segments suspended around the shrine. Kurt gives the player a handcrafted disruptor capable of opening one seam, but only from inside the field.

The player and rival battle Proton and Ariana. They win, damage the cage, and reach the shrine. For a moment, the anomalies stop.

Then Celebi appears.

Celebi does not arrive dramatically. It appears exhausted, moving between injured plants and frightened Pokémon, restoring each one. The player sees that Rocket's entire operation was built around certainty that Celebi would choose compassion.

Voss activates the cage. The damaged seam allows Celebi to resist longer than expected, but the heroes cannot reach every anchor. Proton increases the regional wound, forcing Celebi to spend power healing even as it tries to escape.

The player defeats Voss's containment team, but Giovanni speaks through the system and orders the capture completed. Celebi is pulled into a temporal capsule. Before it disappears, it touches the player's hand. The screen briefly shows the player lying defeated beneath Cinnabar—an image from the future.

Rocket withdraws with Celebi. The player has won every battle in the forest and still lost the objective.

The immediate aftermath matters. Johto's timeline stabilizes, but not perfectly. A few NPCs retain incompatible memories. Elm warns that Celebi's removal has left the repaired timeline fragile.

### Chapter 12: The Cost of Continuity — 2 hours

The alliance meets at Indigo Plateau. Elm explains that Celebi's channel will allow Mewtwo to anticipate attacks, recover from injuries by reverting to earlier states, and potentially choose favorable outcomes from nearby timelines.

Kris recognizes that the cage seam damaged by the player remains a weakness. Ethan notes that Celebi touched the player deliberately. These details become the reason Celebi can later break free after the forced Mewtwo loss.

Red and Leaf first report that Rocket recovered Kanto's Mew-derived culture beneath Cinnabar and activated the Genesis channel. The Johto player sees the final moments through a short playable support operation at Seafoam, arriving in time to rescue Blaine and Fuji but not stop the transfer.

News then arrives that Rocket has captured Jirachi in Hoenn. The Johto player participates in a short playable support sequence at Mossdeep but arrives after the capture. Wally secretly shows the player a single blank crystalline tag Jirachi created before being taken.

Petrel's leaked routing cipher points to Sevii.

## Johto Route Character Resolution

The Johto protagonist begins by believing the past is a source of identity and guidance. The route reveals that memory is neither perfectly reliable nor automatically sacred. Traditions protect people only when living people choose compassion over performance.

Elm's immediate disclosure contrasts with Oak's secrecy. It causes fear and professional damage, but it also gives Johto time to evacuate. Silver's final choice to save Kurt rather than pursue Proton proves that he is no longer living in reaction to Giovanni.

The player cannot prevent Celebi's capture, but the seam opened in the Continuity Cage and the bond formed at the shrine make the final time reversal possible.


# Regional Campaign III — Hoenn Origin

## Core Theme

Hoenn is a story about hope becoming a resource. The region has survived ecological catastrophe and rebuilt through cooperation, science, and optimism. Rocket exploits that optimism, treating wishes as measurable energy and human longing as something that can be harvested. The campaign begins bright and practical, then becomes unsettling as ordinary hopes are recorded, amplified, and converted into power.

The Hoenn campaign is designed for approximately **39 hours**.

## Primary Cast

- **Player:** Brendan or May.
- **Primary rival/partner:** the unselected Hoenn protagonist.
- **Regional professor:** Birch.
- **Recurring Hoenn allies:** Wally, Roxanne, Brawly, Flannery, Norman, Winona, Tate, Liza, Juan, Steven, Wattson, Captain Stern, Professor Cozmo, Archie, Maxie.
- **First foreign-region allies:** Red, Leaf, Oak, Blue, Sabrina, Bill.
- **Second foreign-region allies:** Ethan, Kris, Elm, Silver, Kurt, the Kimono Girls.

## Act I — A Normal Journey Interrupted

### Chapter 1: Littleroot Field Season — 1.5 hours

The player returns to Littleroot after several years away. Brendan has been conducting ecological surveys; May has been leading expeditions for Birch. The selected character has become respected but feels that adulthood turned exploration into schedules, reports, and obligations.

Birch is beginning a new regional study of Pokémon migration following years of climate recovery. He asks the player to build a new team, renew League credentials, and record unusual behavior across Hoenn. The unselected protagonist becomes the main rival and partner, eager to prove that they have not become merely an assistant to Birch.

The opening through Petalburg and toward Rustboro remains recognizably Pokémon. Wally appears only indirectly at first through townspeople who discuss his work helping new trainers. Norman treats the player's return without sentimentality: past reputation does not count as current discipline.

### Chapter 2: The Devon Robbery — 2.5 hours

The player reaches Rustboro and sees several thieves running from Devon Corporation with a sealed case. A Devon employee calls for help. The player battles one thief near the building and wins, but Proton's team escapes using smoke, construction routes, and a waiting cargo vehicle.

The scene remains grounded. No one knows what the case contains. Roxanne organizes the city response while Devon secures the building. The rival arrives after planning to challenge the Gym and asks what happened.

Devon explains only that the stolen device is an **energy-focusing core** intended to stabilize unusual power sources. It was not a weapon and was not associated publicly with any legendary Pokémon. Devon does not deputize the player or ask for the property back.

The player and rival return to Birch. He is alarmed because Rocket should not be operating openly in Hoenn. He contacts Oak, Elm, Devon, and trusted researchers without making a public accusation. He asks the protagonists to continue their journey while observing research sites, power systems, weather stations, meteor studies, and unusual Pokémon behavior.

Birch gives them separate support assignments:

- The male protagonist travels to Mauville to speak with Wattson.
- The female protagonist travels to Slateport to speak with Captain Stern.

Whichever character the player selected handles one lead while the rival handles the other.

The Rustboro Gym challenge can occur before or after the Littleroot report. Roxanne's role remains independent from the crime, preserving the natural League rhythm.

### Chapter 3: Two Quiet Systems — 3.5 hours

In Slateport, Stern asks the assigned protagonist to examine shipping documents and speak with harbor workers. They discover false company names, unusual refrigerated cargo, and repeated requests for routes that avoid mainland League inspections. Nothing yet proves a mythic plan.

In Mauville, Wattson asks the assigned protagonist to speak with the Bike Shop owner, Casino employees, utility workers, and maintenance contractors. Each remembers a power fluctuation lasting only seconds. Individually the interruptions seemed meaningless. Together they form a deliberate charging schedule.

After the local task, the player reports to Stern or Wattson. The rival calls on the PokéNav and asks to meet in Verdanturf.

The player meets the rival and Wally. Wally is now a strong, trusted trainer, not a fragile beginner. He has been helping residents whose Pokémon became agitated during the power fluctuations. He suggests bringing both reports to Wattson.

Wattson concludes that someone has been testing Hoenn's energy network without drawing enough power to trigger emergency systems. He provides access and Surf authorization for New Mauville. Wally remains above ground to help Wattson check the city while the player and rival investigate the station.

Inside New Mauville, the protagonists discover Rocket siphoning power into portable storage cells. The system includes an empty slot perfectly shaped for the stolen Devon core. A recording from Voss describes a coming test of **Desire Resonance**.

The player defeats the local Rocket team, but they remotely drain the stored energy and abandon the equipment. Wally prevents a transformer failure from injuring workers. Birch now accepts that the Devon robbery and power theft are parts of one project.

## Act II — Kanto's Resonance Lens

### Chapter 4: Saffron Conversion — 4.5 hours

Serial numbers in New Mauville trace to a defunct Silph subsidiary. Birch contacts Oak, and the player and rival travel to Kanto. Red and Leaf meet them in Saffron with Blue and Sabrina.

The Hoenn pair expects Kanto's famous heroes to have answers. Instead, they find a region divided by secrecy. Oak will not initially explain why Silph conversion systems might connect to old Cinnabar research. Blue openly accuses him of protecting reputation.

The four protagonists investigate a laboratory beneath a medical equipment company. The facility contains **resonance lenses** capable of translating one form of unusual energy into another. Devon's stolen core is not the power source; it is the focusing mechanism that allows a lens to hold unstable output.

Sabrina tests a lens and hears thousands of overlapping human desires: recovery, revenge, love, money, another chance. The system has been recording emotional energy from public communications and Pokémon Center telemetry. Rocket is not merely studying electricity. It is learning how intention affects psychic energy.

Petrel, disguised as a safety inspector, attempts to frame Devon for illegal research. Leaf exposes inconsistencies in the corporate records, while Red leads the battle through the lower facility. The player and rival recover a partial OMNIS chart naming the channels Genesis, Continuity, Desire, and Adaptation.

Mr. Fuji and Oak identify Genesis as related to Mewtwo. Elm remotely warns that Continuity may be linked to time anomalies emerging in Johto. Birch realizes Desire is Hoenn's channel.

The player asks why Jirachi is not named directly. Sabrina answers that Rocket may not yet know whether the legend is real. The frightening part is that they are building the extraction system before confirming the victim exists.

Before the Hoenn pair leaves, Oak admits that Kanto has evidence Giovanni may have recaptured Mewtwo. The player's regional crime is now part of a threat affecting all three regions.

## Act III — Weather Made Personal

### Chapter 5: The Mountain Takes a Breath — 3.5 hours

Back in Hoenn, Professor Cozmo reports stolen meteor fragments near Fallarbor. The player, rival, and Wally investigate Meteor Falls. Several wild Pokémon are migrating away from a chamber that pulses during moments of strong emotion.

Cozmo explains an old account of a star Pokémon that awakens for a brief period and grants wishes. Birch resists naming Jirachi because he fears turning folklore into a target. Wally argues that Rocket is already acting as if the target is real.

At Mt. Chimney, Rocket installs a lens in the volcanic monitoring network. Flannery, now serving as Mauville's Gym Leader while Wattson handles infrastructure, joins the operation. Maxie and Archie arrive under League supervision because they recognize the machinery as an evolution of their own attempts to force nature into ideological plans.

Maxie says Giovanni's promise of rational control is the same lie he once told himself. Archie says noble motives become irrelevant when everyone else loses the right to refuse.

Rocket triggers a controlled eruption warning, creating panic in Lavaridge and surrounding routes. The lens records thousands of simultaneous wishes for safety. The protagonists shut down the device and defeat Proton, but the captured data transfers away.

This is the first clear revelation that Rocket is using disaster to generate emotional energy.

The player completes the Mauville Gym challenge against Flannery, whose battle philosophy emphasizes pressure without loss of control. The earlier Dewford chapter and Brawly challenge remain part of the ordinary journey between Rustboro and Slateport. Norman's Petalburg challenge follows later as a personal test of whether the player is rebuilding a life or merely reacting to crisis.

### Chapter 6: The Weather Institute Listens — 3.5 hours

Weather patterns begin changing around Fortree in response to public anxiety rather than atmospheric conditions. When residents fear rain, clouds gather. When they hope for clear skies, storms hesitate. The effects are small but impossible.

At the Weather Institute, Brendan identifies Pokémon migration that anticipates emotional events rather than weather. May discovers Rocket survey teams interviewing communities under the pretense of disaster preparedness. The surveys ask what residents would change if given one wish.

The player, rival, Wally, Winona, and institute staff defend the facility from Ariana. Rocket steals a model combining weather data with regional population sentiment. The protagonists save the main systems but lose the predictive model.

Birch admits Devon incorporated his environmental work into the stolen core. His research can predict where a Jirachi awakening would encounter the strongest concentration of human desire.

Wally reveals that one Rocket survey asked whether he would trade his current life to have been born healthy. He refused to answer. He says the question frightened him because a younger version of himself might have said yes.

The Fortree Gym challenge occurs after the defense. Winona describes flight as constant correction, not freedom from resistance. Tate and Liza later conduct the Mossdeep challenge after the Space Center is secured.

### Chapter 7: Sootopolis Without a Miracle — 3 hours

Small crowds begin gathering in Sootopolis after rumors spread that a wish-granting Pokémon will awaken. Ariana's public fronts deliberately leak contradictory stories, causing desperate people to travel, spend money, and make themselves vulnerable.

Juan and Steven work to prevent panic. The player investigates a clinic funded by an Ariana charity. The clinic provides real help, which makes shutting it down morally complicated. Beneath it, Rocket records the wishes of patients and families.

The player discovers that Ariana recruits by offering practical solutions where the League has failed. Some employees are not aware of the extraction project. The story allows the player to distinguish beneficiaries, coerced workers, and committed Rocket personnel rather than treating everyone as identical.

Ariana argues that hope is already bought and sold by corporations, politicians, and trainers. Rocket is merely honest about the transaction. Wally rejects her framing: helping someone to own their gratitude is not help.

The facility is closed without abandoning its patients; Birch and Juan arrange replacement care. The player completes Juan's Sootopolis challenge once the patients are safe. Wallace, now leading a Lilycove performance-oriented Gym, provides the eighth Hoenn badge before the final Mossdeep countdown. The protagonists recover a map pointing toward Mossdeep and a series of meteor alignments.

## Act IV — Johto's Language of Wishes

### Chapter 8: Ecruteak Paper — 4.5 hours

Elm asks Birch to send the Hoenn protagonists to Johto. Unown patterns appearing near Ecruteak match markings found on the meteor fragments. The player and rival travel with Wally and meet Ethan, Kris, and Silver.

The crossover explores how wishes and time interact. The Kimono Girls explain that many traditional wishes are not requests for reality to obey. They are promises about how the person making the wish intends to live. A wish written for another person carries less selfish distortion.

Kris compares Hoenn star-festival tags with Johto charms used to remember the dead and guide future choices. Kurt experiments with meteor dust and Apricorn fibers, producing a paperlike crystalline strip that can retain psychic intent without immediately releasing it. He calls it a **Wish Tag**, though he considers it ceremonial rather than powerful.

Rocket attacks Ecruteak to steal the prototype. During the battle, a temporal anomaly causes the player to see several possible outcomes: Wally as Champion, Wally dying young, Wally never leaving home, and Wally serving Rocket in exchange for health. Wally rejects all of them as lives chosen for him by fear.

The heroes save Kurt's notes, but Rocket steals several imperfect tags and Unown encoding patterns. Voss now has a way to formulate a wish as a machine instruction.

At Ilex Forest, the player witnesses early signs of the Continuity operation. Celebi is not seen, but damaged plants heal overnight and a shrine visitor remembers meeting the player in Kanto. Ethan and Kris remain behind to investigate.

Before the Hoenn group leaves, Kurt gives Wally one blank Wish Tag. He says it will only hold meaning if the person using it accepts that a wish cannot own the being granting it.

## Act V — Seven Days of Jirachi

### Chapter 9: Mossdeep Countdown — 3.5 hours

Tate and Liza detect a psychic signal approaching Hoenn with a recurring meteor shower. Cozmo calculates that the signal will peak for seven days. Birch finally names Jirachi publicly to the alliance but keeps the information from mass media to prevent further panic.

Rocket assaults the Mossdeep Space Center, not to take control of a rocket but to use its tracking arrays and weather satellites as a regional focusing system. The player, rival, Wally, Steven, Tate, and Liza defend the center.

The heroes repel the assault, but Petrel uploads a hidden program planted months earlier. Every major weather and communications station begins collecting emotional telemetry. The Devon core, installed at a remote site, focuses the data toward Meteor Falls.

Tate and Liza hear a sleeping presence begin to dream. Jirachi's dreams briefly affect reality: lost objects appear near their owners, old messages arrive, and small childhood wishes come true in incomplete ways. The moments are beautiful at first, then unsettling when contradictory wishes collide.

### Chapter 10: The Wishes People Regret — 3 hours

The player travels across several Hoenn communities during Jirachi's first days awake. This chapter is structured like a traditional Pokémon route sequence but tied to human stories.

- A fisherman wishes for endless catches, then watches the local ecosystem collapse in a possible vision.
- A grieving parent wishes to hear a lost child's voice and receives only an old recording.
- A trainer wishes never to lose and finds that no one will battle them.
- A Rocket grunt wishes to leave the organization but fears what happens to their family.

Jirachi does not fully grant these wishes. Its dreams reflect them, revealing consequences. Wally becomes the first person to calm an affected Pokémon by asking what it needs rather than what he wants.

Birch concludes that Jirachi is moving toward the strongest concentration of unselfish desire, not the loudest wish. Rocket therefore creates a disaster near Meteor Falls, expecting the population's wish for survival to pull Jirachi into the lens.

### Chapter 11: Starfall Crater — 5 hours

Rocket sabotages weather controls, power relays, and mountain supports around Meteor Falls. The resulting storm threatens Fallarbor, Route 114, and the cave settlements. Red, Leaf, Ethan, and Kris arrive with Oak and Elm as the crisis becomes interregional. Steven, Winona, Archie, Maxie, Flannery, and Norman coordinate evacuations while the six protagonists divide across the region.

The player, rival, and Wally enter **Starfall Crater**, an ancient chamber beneath Meteor Falls where meteor fragments form a natural resonance field. Rocket has installed the stolen Devon core at its center and surrounded it with imperfect Wish Tags encoded as commands:

- awaken;
- approach;
- remain;
- grant;
- obey.

The player battles Proton and Voss while the rival disables weather relays. Wally reaches the natural chamber and sees Jirachi awaken, frightened and disoriented.

Wally is given a clear opportunity to make a personal wish. Jirachi shows him a life without illness, fear, or limitation. He refuses. He tells Jirachi that the life he has is not a mistake that needs correcting.

Jirachi creates one genuine crystalline Wish Tag and gives it to Wally. The tag is blank because trust cannot be prewritten.

The player defeats Voss, but Giovanni activates the Devon core remotely. Rocket's machine uses the entire region's wish for the storm to end as a binding command. Jirachi spends its first act of freedom saving the people outside, leaving it too weak to resist the containment field.

The player and rival destroy most of the encoded tags. Wally uses Kurt's blank ceremonial tag to open a gap, but Ariana arrives with a perfected containment unit. Jirachi is captured.

Before the capsule closes, Jirachi presses the genuine crystalline tag into Wally's hand. Rocket does not detect it.

The storm ends. To the public, the crisis appears to have been an equipment failure contained by the League. The heroes know that Hoenn's collective hope was harvested to imprison the Pokémon that answered it.

### Chapter 12: A Wish With No Owner — 2.5 hours

Before the Mossdeep meeting, Red and Leaf report that Rocket activated the Genesis channel beneath Cinnabar, while Ethan and Kris report Celebi's capture after the Ilex Forest wound. The Hoenn player and rival assist in brief aftermath sequences in both regions, reinforcing that the losses were costly battles rather than off-screen announcements.

At Mossdeep, all six protagonists and all three professors meet physically for the first time in the Hoenn route. Wally reveals the genuine Wish Tag. Tate and Liza sense that it can carry one precisely worded wish if Jirachi willingly completes it.

The group debates using it immediately to free Jirachi. Kris warns that the tag has no power while Jirachi is contained and cannot consent. Oak argues it must be protected for the larger threat. Wally refuses to let any institution claim ownership and keeps it with the player as co-custodian.

The alliance establishes the complete model:

- Genesis allows Mewtwo's body to accept the other channels.
- Continuity allows it to reverse damage and select favorable outcomes.
- Desire allows commands and imagined states to become real.
- Adaptation will allow its body and mind to mutate around any remaining limit.

Petrel's leaked cipher points to Sevii. Stern confirms that the stolen Devon core and Jirachi capsule were shipped through an island route outside mainland oversight.

## Hoenn Route Character Resolution

The Hoenn protagonist begins by wanting exploration to feel hopeful again. The route does not reject hope; it rejects the idea that hope is valuable only when it produces a desired outcome.

Birch learns that withholding frightening information does not protect people. Wally's refusal to rewrite his own life earns Jirachi's trust. The genuine Wish Tag becomes the story's most important object because it was created through consent rather than extraction.

The player cannot stop Jirachi's capture, but destroys the command tags, preserves the genuine tag, and proves that a wish can be a promise rather than an order.


# Shared Interregional Story

The following chapters occur in all three origin campaigns. Dialogue and tactical emphasis change according to the selected protagonist, but the core events remain consistent.

# The Omnis Alliance

## Alliance Summit — 1 hour

The six protagonists, three professors, Blue, Silver, Wally, Steven, Sabrina, Bill, and selected regional leaders gather at Indigo Plateau under emergency authority. The meeting is tense rather than celebratory.

Blue accuses Oak, Elm, Birch, Silph, and Devon of allowing professional secrecy to divide evidence that should have been shared years earlier. Steven accepts responsibility for Devon's weak oversight. Elm argues that immediate public disclosure may cause panic around the remaining targets. Lorelei joins remotely from Sevii and says mainland officials already caused the greater danger by treating the islands as peripheral.

The six protagonists compare what they personally witnessed. This scene allows the player to hear concise versions of the two regional climaxes not played in full. No one hero has the entire story.

The alliance assigns functional roles:

- **Red:** frontline combat and high-risk pursuit.
- **Leaf:** evidence synthesis, infiltration planning, and civilian logistics.
- **Ethan:** route security, evacuation, and field coordination.
- **Kris:** Unown, ritual structures, and temporal analysis.
- **Brendan:** environmental tracking and adaptive terrain planning.
- **May:** expedition command, communications, and team coordination.
- **Blue:** League forces and strategic command.
- **Silver:** Rocket doctrine and internal habits.
- **Wally:** Jirachi liaison and Wish Tag custodian.
- **Oak:** Genesis channel and Mewtwo history.
- **Elm:** Continuity channel and temporal stability.
- **Birch:** Desire channel and environmental resonance.

The alliance does not become perfectly harmonious. Silver distrusts Oak. Blue thinks the professors are still speaking too cautiously. Red dislikes being treated as the obvious weapon. Kris and Sabrina disagree over whether psychic visions are evidence. Wally refuses to surrender the Wish Tag to League custody.

Bill and Celio decode Petrel's routing cipher. It identifies a chain across One, Four, Five, Six, and Seven Islands, but the final destination is omitted.

# Shared Sevii Arc — 4.5 to 5 hours

## Chapter 1: One Island Blackout

The alliance travels to One Island in staggered groups to avoid revealing its full strength. Celio's Network Center is attacked minutes before the player arrives. Rocket is not trying to destroy the system; it is erasing records of years of encrypted traffic.

The selected player and local rival fight through the center with two protagonists from another region. This rotating-party structure allows all six to work with one another rather than remaining in regional pairs.

Bill restores part of the archive and finds that Rocket has used Sevii as a neutral transfer zone since before Giovanni's public return. Genetic material from Kanto, radio components from Johto, and power systems from Hoenn all passed through island companies with legitimate licenses.

Celio is furious that mainland investigators repeatedly dismissed unusual traffic as island smuggling. Lorelei arrives and assumes operational control of local defense.

A recovered packet points to Icefall Cave on Four Island.

## Chapter 2: Icefall Archive

Rocket converted a deep section of Icefall Cave into cold storage. The alliance finds failed containment prototypes, damaged meteor material, abandoned tissue cultures, and records of Pokémon injured during adaptive testing.

The facility explains why Deoxys is needed. The first three channels create overwhelming power but also instability:

- Genesis keeps the body alive.
- Continuity repairs damage.
- Desire imposes intended outcomes.
- Without Adaptation, Mewtwo's mind and body would eventually fracture under incompatible forces.

Deoxys is not an extra weapon. It is the mechanism that will allow Mewtwo to change faster than the stolen powers can destroy it.

Lorelei discovers a list of island residents paid to ignore shipments. Some did so knowingly; others believed the cargo was medical research. She refuses Blue's suggestion of mass arrests and insists on distinguishing coercion from collaboration.

A surviving scientist says Rocket's adaptive containment was tested at Five Island Warehouse.

## Chapter 3: Five Island Warehouse

The warehouse is now a full black site hidden beneath the familiar surface facility. The player and rival enter with Silver and Leaf or May, while Red and Ethan lead an external assault and Kris and Brendan trace an energy signal.

Inside, the heroes find Petrel wounded and abandoned. Archer ordered his execution after discovering the leaked cipher. Petrel offers the final routing key in exchange for extraction. Silver wants to leave him; the player can challenge the impulse but the story does not pretend Petrel is redeemed.

Petrel reveals:

- Cinnabar is already occupied below ground.
- Giovanni has Mewtwo in a psychic suspension chamber.
- The mythic capsules are being shipped separately to prevent a single interception.
- Rocket is searching for an extraterrestrial organism first observed through signals near Six Island.
- Archer intends to destroy the Sevii network after the final transfer, erasing evidence and stranding island communities.

The alliance shuts down demolition charges. Petrel is taken into custody, still bargaining for leniency.

Ariana confronts the player during the escape. She argues that mainland heroes only care about Sevii because it now matters to their crisis. Lorelei agrees with the accusation but rejects the conclusion: neglect does not give Rocket ownership.

The next clue lies in Dotted Hole on Six Island.

## Chapter 4: Six Island Revelation

For most of the Sevii arc, the player believes the islands are only Rocket's logistics chain. Six Island reveals the final target.

Kris, Sabrina, Cozmo, Bill, and Celio combine Braille inscriptions, Unown spacing, psychic impressions, and network coordinates. The Dotted Hole markings describe a visitor that changes shape in response to threat. A meteor fragment stored nearby reacts by forming a triangular pattern.

Brendan tracks unusual Pokémon migration away from the southern sea. May discovers that Rocket vessels have been searching an area excluded from standard charts. Celio identifies the coordinate as **Birth Island**.

The alliance plans an interception. Oak argues that destroying Rocket's vessels may be safer than attempting contact with Deoxys. Birch objects that no one knows whether Deoxys is hostile. Silver notes that Rocket will force a confrontation regardless.

The group uses Seven Island's Trainer Tower as a temporary signal platform to open a route through unstable weather. Rocket attacks the tower, producing a large multi-character defense in which each protagonist protects one system. The player defeats Archer, but he escapes by helicopter to Birth Island.

## Chapter 5: Birth Island

Birth Island should feel alien, quiet, and geometrically wrong. The environment reacts to movement. Paths appear where the player did not walk, and the central triangle changes position when not observed.

The six protagonists approach in three pairs. The player and local rival reach the center first. Deoxys appears defensive rather than malicious, changing forms in response to perceived threats.

The player completes a battle intended to communicate restraint: surviving and reducing Deoxys's panic rather than capturing it. Just as contact becomes possible, Archer activates Rocket's adaptive lattice around the island.

The containment system is designed to learn from every attempt to break it. Red's force, Sabrina's psychic counterpressure, Kris's pattern disruption, and Brendan's environmental sabotage each work once before the lattice adapts.

The player and rival reach the central control and defeat Archer. However, Dr. Voss uses the protagonists' own attacks as the final training data. The lattice changes into a configuration none of them have used.

Deoxys is captured.

Before the capsule closes, Deoxys sends the player a fragmented image: Mewtwo suspended beneath Cinnabar, surrounded by four lights, and Giovanni standing outside the chamber.

Rocket launches the capsule toward Cinnabar using an automated high-altitude craft. The alliance cannot intercept it without risking the capsule's destruction.

Birth Island begins destabilizing. The heroes choose evacuation over pursuit and save Rocket personnel as well as allies. Archer escapes separately, but his command over Sevii collapses.

The four channels are now complete.

# Shared Finale — Cinnabar and Faraway Island

## Chapter 1: The Occupied Island — 1.5 hours

Cinnabar is revealed to have been occupied from below for months. Rocket raises defensive structures through the ruins of the Pokémon Mansion, jams maritime routes, and announces that the island contains hazardous material to justify a civilian exclusion zone.

The alliance launches a three-front assault:

- **Surface team:** Blue, Lorelei, Steven, regional Gym Leaders, and League forces secure the coast and prevent Rocket evacuation.
- **Systems team:** Bill, Celio, Oak, Elm, Birch, Sabrina, and two of the visiting protagonists attack power, temporal, and communications systems.
- **Core team:** the selected player, local rival, the other two visiting protagonists, Silver, and Wally descend through the Mansion and laboratories.

The assignments change slightly by origin route. All six protagonists reunite at the Siphon chamber before the final release sequence. The assault contains familiar Pokémon adventure elements—trainer battles, environmental puzzles, rival coordination—but the atmosphere is a siege.

The old Mansion transitions into a modern citadel. Rocket has preserved portions of the original laboratory like religious relics. Giovanni's organization claims to reject superstition while treating the Mewtwo project as sacred history.

The player sees Mewtwo in suspension. It is conscious.

## Chapter 2: Giovanni's Answer

Giovanni addresses the alliance from the Mythic Siphon chamber. He does not deny the regional damage. He argues that every government accepts casualties to create security and that the Leagues have repeatedly done the same by sending trainers into disasters.

He describes Mewtwo as the only possible answer to a world where ancient Pokémon can reshape oceans, continents, weather, minds, and time. A being with all four channels could stop any future catastrophe before it begins.

Oak says a guardian created without consent is only a hostage with enough power to become a tyrant. Giovanni replies that consent is a luxury the threatened cannot always afford.

The player defeats Giovanni's final team. Giovanni does not appear shocked. The battle supplied the last stress data Voss needed.

Voss activates the Mythic Siphon:

- the Genesis channel expands Mewtwo's genetic anchor;
- the Continuity channel floods its awareness with adjacent moments;
- the Desire channel forces imagined outcomes into physical reality;
- the Adaptation channel reshapes it around every failure.

Mewtwo awakens larger in presence rather than grotesque in form. Its body flickers between possible states. Attacks miss because it remembers futures where they were aimed differently. Wounds reverse. The room changes in response to anger.

Giovanni commands Mewtwo to kneel.

Mewtwo looks at him and says that every version of Giovanni makes the same mistake.

A psychic flash consumes Giovanni. He vanishes without a body, leaving it unclear whether he was killed, displaced in time, trapped in Mewtwo's mind, or thrown somewhere else.

Voss loses control of the system. Mewtwo turns on everyone.

## Chapter 3: The Battle That Cannot Be Won — 1 hour

The player battles empowered Mewtwo. The encounter must communicate impossibility without feeling like an ordinary unfair level spike.

During the battle:

- the first successful status condition is removed by Continuity;
- the first super-effective attack causes Adaptation to change Mewtwo's defenses;
- healing may be undone by Desire, returning the player's team to an injured state;
- defeated allied Pokémon appear to stand again, then collapse as the timeline corrects;
- Mewtwo responds to party bonds with increasing anger because they represent relationships it was denied.

The player may survive several turns or reduce a visible stability meter, but cannot win. Mewtwo eventually uses **Omnis Break**, a psychic event that defeats the party and begins tearing the citadel apart.

Oak, Elm, and Birch attempt to release the mythics. The damaged seam in Celebi's cage—created during the Johto climax—allows Celebi to extend one hand through the field. It recognizes the player from Ilex Forest and from the future image it previously shared.

Celebi breaks free for a few seconds and sends the player backward through time.

The screen goes white before the outcome of the first assault is shown.

## Chapter 4: The Day Outside Time — 1.5 hours

The player awakens on the morning before the Cinnabar assault, carrying a green temporal mark and complete memory of the failed attack. Their party remembers only as emotion and trust, avoiding a long explanation of physical paradox.

The player immediately contacts the rival and professors. Elm verifies the temporal mark. Oak believes the account because the player knows details of the hidden chamber no one has seen. The alliance begins revising the assault.

However, no tactical change solves the central problem. As long as all four channels empower Mewtwo, every ordinary strategy becomes another pattern Deoxys's power can adapt around.

Celebi's mark contains an image of Faraway Island. Mr. Fuji explains an old theory: Mew's psychic relationship to Mewtwo is not simply genetic. Mew represents unrestricted possibility, while Mewtwo represents possibility forced into a designed purpose. Mew may be the only being Mewtwo cannot reduce to a predictable opponent.

Faraway Island is absent from current navigation systems. Stern, Bill, Celio, and the regional professors combine old sea charts, temporal coordinates, and Mew's previous Cinnabar appearance to locate a route that exists only during a narrow tide.

The player and rival travel alone at first, preserving the island's intimacy. The route passes through unnaturally calm water. No Rocket trainers appear.

Faraway Island contains no laboratory and no grand shrine. It is ancient, overgrown, and indifferent to human history. Mew watches the player from a distance, testing whether they pursue, wait, protect wild Pokémon, or attempt to control the encounter.

The player does not capture Mew. Instead, the temporal mark shows Mew the first timeline: Mewtwo's pain, the mythics in cages, and the collapse beneath Cinnabar. Mew also sees that Mewtwo made Giovanni vanish before attacking anyone else. It recognizes both danger and suffering.

Mew chooses to help.

It touches the player's starter and shares a brief image of the first bond between them. The scene reinforces that the answer to engineered power is not greater ownership but freely chosen connection.

## Chapter 5: The Second Assault — 1.5 hours

The second assault repeats only enough to create dread and payoff. Approximately the first twenty minutes mirror the original operation with shortened dialogue. The player now changes specific decisions:

1. The alliance attacks the temporal cage first, widening Celebi's damaged seam.
2. Brendan and Kris seed the Adaptation system with contradictory environmental and symbolic data.
3. Leaf and May reroute civilians and prisoners before the citadel locks down.
4. Ethan and Silver capture Proton before he can activate emergency damage protocols.
5. Blue and Red prevent Archer from escaping the surface.
6. Wally and the player carry the genuine Wish Tag directly to Jirachi's chamber.

Giovanni still activates the Mythic Siphon. This event behaves like a fixed point because he makes the decision before the altered assault can reach him. The player tries to warn him that Mewtwo will reject him. Giovanni interprets the warning as proof that Mewtwo's future can be known and controlled.

Mewtwo awakens. Giovanni commands it. Mewtwo makes him vanish again.

The psychic wave spreads toward the alliance. Oak steps between Mewtwo and the core team, using a damaged control interface to redirect the wave. For an instant, Oak sees Giovanni in a white, undefined space. Then Oak also vanishes.

This preserves a cost in the corrected timeline. The player saved many people, but knowledge of the future did not create a perfect ending.

Mew arrives before Mewtwo can use Omnis Break.

## Chapter 6: Mew and Mewtwo

Mew and Mewtwo battle as autonomous characters in the background and foreground of the final sequence. The confrontation should not reduce to a normal player-controlled legendary battle.

Mew is initially weaker in raw force, but Mewtwo cannot predict it. Mew changes tactics without seeking dominance, imitates ordinary Pokémon moves, hides, plays, protects allies, and refuses the logic of escalation. Adaptation can respond to attacks but not to purposeless creativity.

The player and rival fight through the collapsing Siphon while the other protagonists free the mythics:

- Ethan and Kris release Celebi through the widened seam.
- Brendan and May disable Jirachi's regional focusing core.
- Red and Leaf break Deoxys's adaptive lattice using simultaneous contradictory attacks.
- Silver holds off remaining Rocket elites.
- Blue, Steven, and Lorelei keep the facility from collapsing into the sea.

Celebi escapes immediately and stabilizes the citadel long enough for evacuation. Deoxys escapes immediately, shifting forms and disappearing into the upper atmosphere. Neither becomes capturable in the main story.

Jirachi remains weak and frightened. Wally approaches without asking it to grant anything. He gives the genuine Wish Tag to the player and says the wording must not command Jirachi or destroy Mewtwo.

The player writes or speaks the wish:

**“Return what was taken. Leave what was always theirs.”**

Jirachi accepts.

The Wish Tag does not remove Mewtwo's natural power, memories, or identity. It separates the stolen Continuity, Desire, and Adaptation channels and releases the expanded Genesis material from forced activation.

The energy returns to its sources:

- Celebi's temporal power repairs the worst remaining fractures.
- Jirachi's wish energy disperses through Hoenn as harmless falling light.
- Deoxys's adaptive energy follows it into the sky.
- The Mew-derived culture becomes inert rather than creating another living weapon.

Mewtwo becomes vulnerable, exhausted, and fully itself.

Mew overpowers it but refuses to kill it. Mewtwo sees the freed mythics and the humans protecting one another without ownership. It accuses Mew of abandoning it to human cruelty. Mew answers through images rather than speech: not innocence, but invitation.

Mewtwo rejects the invitation for now. It escapes through the collapsing upper chamber. Mew follows long enough to prevent pursuit, then also disappears.

The player never captures either Pokémon.

## Chapter 7: Cinnabar Dawn — 30–45 minutes

The citadel collapses into sealed lower caverns. League forces rescue surviving Rocket personnel and researchers. Voss is arrested, though she continues insisting the experiment proved mythic traits can be separated and engineered. Archer is captured on the surface. Ariana's fate varies slightly by route but she escapes during the evacuation, creating a postgame Rocket thread. Proton remains in custody. Petrel enters a formal cooperation agreement without being forgiven.

Oak is missing. No body or stable psychic trace is found. Giovanni is also missing. Sabrina senses both men at different moments in the same unreachable white space.

The professors issue a joint public statement admitting their research and secrecy contributed to the crisis. The Leagues establish an interregional oversight body, but the ending avoids presenting bureaucracy as a complete solution.

The six protagonists meet on Cinnabar's shore at sunrise. Their old public identities—Champion, prodigy, researcher, survivor—matter less than the network they have created.

The local rival gives the player the final battle of the main story after a short recovery period, not to determine who saved the world but to complete the ordinary journey that Rocket interrupted. The battle can occur at Indigo Plateau or a restored regional location depending on origin.

The credits show:

- Celebi moving through Ilex Forest as contradictory memories settle.
- Jirachi sleeping again, with one blank Wish Tag beside it.
- Deoxys watching the planet from orbit.
- Mew appearing briefly near children playing on Faraway Island.
- Mewtwo standing alone in an unknown ruin, hearing distant voices it cannot identify.
- Oak walking through a white corridor and finding a black hat on the ground.

# Origin-Specific Finale Emphasis

## Kanto Origin

The finale emphasizes responsibility for Mewtwo. Red or Leaf confronts Oak before the second assault and asks whether he would hide the truth again. Oak says fear made secrecy feel like protection. His disappearance prevents easy reconciliation.

The Kanto rival is the first to identify that Mewtwo's rage is directed less at humanity in general than at every person who speaks about it as an outcome. The route closes with Kanto beginning a public memorial and archive for the victims of the Cinnabar project.

## Johto Origin

The finale emphasizes the cost of changing time. Ethan or Kris knows the second timeline may erase memories and relationships created in the first. Celebi does not restore a perfect past; it permits one additional choice.

The Johto rival uses the Sprout Tower bell sequence to help stabilize Celebi during the final release. Several NPCs retain dreams of the failed timeline, preserving consequence rather than resetting everything cleanly.

## Hoenn Origin

The finale emphasizes the wording and ethics of the wish. Brendan or May carries the Wish Tag with Wally and must reject more tempting formulations, such as destroying Mewtwo, restoring every loss, or bringing Oak back immediately.

The final wish succeeds because it returns stolen power without deciding what Mewtwo, Mew, or the mythics should become. Hoenn later holds a star festival where people write promises rather than requests.

# Postgame Story Threads

## The White Corridor

Sabrina, Celebi, and the Unown identify a space outside ordinary sequence where Oak and Giovanni may be trapped. The postgame can explore whether Mewtwo intentionally sent them there, whether Giovanni survived, and whether saving Oak also means releasing Giovanni.

## Mewtwo Sightings

Reports place Mewtwo in abandoned laboratories across all three regions. It attacks Rocket remnants but also steals research related to its own creation. The postgame question is whether it is seeking self-understanding, revenge, or a way to remove the last human influence from its body.

## Ariana's Network

Ariana retains several legitimate companies and communities dependent on them. Defeating her requires replacing services, exposing coercion, and earning trust rather than simply clearing hideouts.

## Legendary Omens

Other legendary Pokémon respond to the temporary imbalance:

- Lugia appears near damaged sea routes but avoids contact.
- Ho-Oh is seen above Ecruteak after memories stabilize.
- Rayquaza observes the energy released from Deoxys but does not descend.
- Groudon and Kyogre stir briefly as Hoenn's wish energy disperses.
- The legendary birds migrate around Cinnabar's altered climate.

These are postgame seeds, not mandatory main-story captures.

## The Six

The six protagonists establish a loose interregional field network independent of any one League or professor. Their postgame missions pair characters who rarely worked together during the main route, allowing continued character development.

# Final Story Principles

- The game should feel like Pokémon before it feels like a thriller.
- Towns, Gyms, routes, rivals, and ordinary Pokémon relationships remain the emotional foundation.
- Adult themes should come from responsibility, exploitation, institutional failure, grief, coercion, and the ethics of control—not from gratuitous violence.
- Rocket's victories must make sense even when the player wins battles.
- The six protagonists should solve different kinds of problems; none should exist only to praise the player.
- The three professors should be essential, flawed, and accountable.
- Mew, Celebi, Jirachi, and Deoxys should choose, resist, fear, and act as characters.
- Giovanni's plan should be understandable but unacceptable.
- The final victory should come from consent, cooperation, and precise sacrifice rather than the player simply possessing a stronger legendary Pokémon.
