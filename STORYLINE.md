# Pokemon Omnis Storyline

This file is for building the main story in simple terms.

Keep this file focused on story flow. Do not add flags, variables, map tasks, or implementation notes here.

## Core Story Points

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Player identity | The player chooses Kanto, Johto, or Hoenn at the start. The selected protagonist is an adult returning to active field work, not a child leaving home. The unchosen regional protagonists still exist as adult allies. |
| Opening tone | The story begins with ordinary regional activity before Team Rocket's larger plan is known. Early Rocket incidents should look like thefts, raids, and industrial crimes instead of obvious mythic events. |
| Giovanni's return | Giovanni has reappeared and rebuilt Team Rocket. His hidden project is a created or empowered Mewtwo, but the heroes do not know this at the start. |
| Mythic Siphon | Giovanni's secret machine is the Mythic Siphon. It draws on four mythic channels: Mew-derived genetics, Celebi's time power, Jirachi's wish power, and Deoxys's mutation power. |
| Kanto channel | Kanto supplies the Mew-derived channel through Cinnabar research, old Mewtwo project data, and surviving genetic residue. Mew itself is never captured by Team Rocket. |
| Johto channel | Johto supplies the temporal channel. Rocket wounds time badly enough that Celebi appears to repair the damage, then captures Celebi in-story. |
| Hoenn channel | Hoenn supplies the wish channel. Rocket turns Devon science, meteor research, weather systems, and wish folklore toward Jirachi. |
| Hoenn Regi role | The Regi trio becomes part of Rocket's Jirachi plan. Regirock helps Rocket locate ancient meteor and wish-energy signatures, Regice teaches Rocket how to disturb a protected thousand-year hibernation, and Registeel gives Rocket the containment breakthrough: a flexible, unbreakable living-metal lattice that can hold Jirachi when its wish power surges. The Regis are not checklist captures; they are ancient tests and anchors Rocket abuses while pursuing Jirachi. |
| Sevii channel | Sevii supplies the mutation channel. Rocket uses the islands as a black-site logistics chain and captures Deoxys at Birth Island. |
| Player role | The player is usually too late to stop each mythic capture, but each regional branch exposes part of the larger plan, weakens Rocket's network, and makes the final assault possible. |
| Cinnabar finale | Cinnabar Island becomes Rocket-occupied late in the story. The finale happens beneath the island, inside a Rocket citadel built through the Pokemon Mansion, lab ruins, and lower facility. |
| First Mewtwo battle | The player's first battle against empowered Mewtwo is impossible to win. The loss proves that no normal team can defeat Mewtwo in this state. |
| Time reversal | Celebi breaks free after the first loss and sends the player back in time. This creates a second chance instead of a simple victory after the first assault. |
| Faraway Island | Faraway Island becomes part of the main story. The player goes there to find Mew because Mew is the only Pokemon capable of standing against Mewtwo directly. |
| Second assault | The player returns to Cinnabar with Mew. The same major events begin to repeat, but this time Mew battles Mewtwo while the player and allies free the captured mythics. |
| Wish Tag payoff | Celebi and Deoxys escape immediately. The player or another major character bonds with Jirachi and uses a Wish Tag to remove Mewtwo's stolen mythic power. |
| Ending | Once the stolen power is removed, Mew overpowers Mewtwo. Mewtwo escapes after being freed from the extra power, and Mew also escapes. |
| Postgame threads | The main story ends with Rocket defeated, the mythics free, Oak missing, Giovanni's fate uncertain, and Mewtwo unresolved. |
| Legendary policy | Legendary Pokemon should mostly appear as omens, guardians, or postgame seeds instead of main-story checklist bosses. |

## Main Story Flow

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Origin | The player starts in Kanto, Johto, or Hoenn. This phase establishes the adult hero, the local professor, the local rival or partner, and the first ordinary goal. |
| First Rocket incident | The chosen starting region eventually reveals Team Rocket through a grounded crime. Kanto uses Pewter Museum, Johto uses Sprout Tower, and Hoenn uses Devon Corp. |
| Regional branches | After the first crisis opens the wider investigation, the player can work through the Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn branches. Each branch reveals one part of Giovanni's machine. |
| Regional merge | Once Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn are resolved, the heroes compare evidence. Kanto explains the vessel science, Johto explains the time channel, and Hoenn explains the wish channel. |
| Sevii arc | The evidence points to Sevii, where Rocket has hidden warehouses, relays, shipping routes, and research sites away from mainland League oversight. |
| Deoxys capture | Birth Island becomes the Sevii climax. Rocket captures Deoxys and completes the fourth mythic channel. |
| First Cinnabar assault | The heroes raid occupied Cinnabar and reach the Rocket citadel. Giovanni activates the Mythic Siphon and empowers Mewtwo. |
| Failed ending | Mewtwo rejects Giovanni, makes him vanish, and defeats the player. This is not the end because Celebi breaks free and sends the player back in time. |
| Faraway Island | The player finds Mew in the past. Mew does not become a captured tool; it chooses to help. |
| Second Cinnabar assault | The player returns with Mew and changes the failed outcome. Mew holds off Mewtwo while the player and allies free the mythics. |
| Final resolution | Jirachi's Wish Tags remove Mewtwo's stolen power. Mew overpowers Mewtwo, Mew and Mewtwo escape, and the main story ends with several postgame threads still open. |

## Branch Overview

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Kanto branch | Kanto is about Giovanni rebuilding the Mewtwo project. The player is not chasing Mew. Rocket is recovering old science, psychic control tech, power infrastructure, and Mew-derived research. Kanto should feel like old Rocket scars being reopened. |
| Johto branch | Johto is about Rocket turning folklore and time damage into a trap for Celebi. Rocket is not simply summoning Celebi. Rocket is wounding the timeline badly enough that Celebi appears to repair it. Johto should feel spiritual, strange, and increasingly wrong. |
| Hoenn branch | Hoenn is about Rocket turning wishes into extractable energy. Rocket is using Devon science, meteor research, weather systems, and ancient Hoenn myth. Hoenn should feel hopeful at first, then unsettling as Rocket treats hope like fuel. |
| Sevii branch | Sevii is the bridge between the regional investigations and the finale. Rocket has used the islands as warehouses, relay stations, decoy docks, and a place to chase extraterrestrial phenomena away from mainland League oversight. |

## Branch 1: Kanto

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Core idea | Kanto is not about chasing Mew. It is about proving Giovanni has rebuilt the old Mewtwo project from stolen research, old Rocket infrastructure, Silph-style control technology, power-grid theft, and a surviving Mew-derived sample. |
| Starting version | The adult Kanto hero returns to Pallet Town. Oak gives the player a normal research errand. Blue or the unchosen Kanto protagonist acts as the local partner. The first Rocket incident is a Pewter Museum theft. At first, the theft looks like a fossil, Moon Stone, or research archive crime. |
| First visible crime | Pewter Museum is robbed during what should have been a routine local errand. Rocket steals fossil restoration data, Moon Stone samples, or old Cinnabar research citations. Brock and the museum staff initially read it as theft, not a mythic plot. |
| Investigation shape | The branch should move through Kanto's old Rocket history. Each stop should reveal another piece of Giovanni's new machine: old command caches, psychic residue, power infrastructure, control technology, shipping ledgers, and Cinnabar research. |
| Emotional center | Kanto needs a moment that makes the Mewtwo project feel morally ugly, not just scientifically dangerous. Mr. Fuji, Blaine, Oak, or another witness should explain the human and Pokemon cost of treating life as a project. |
| Current ending target | Rocket obtains the Mew-derived channel from Cinnabar research. Mew briefly appears as a free witness, not a captive. The alliance learns that Kanto supplied the vessel science for Mewtwo. |
| Questions to build | What is the Kanto hero's adult life before the story begins? What ordinary errand pulls the player toward Pewter? What exactly does Rocket steal from Pewter Museum? When should the player first hear that Giovanni may be back? Which Kanto locations are essential for this branch? How does Kanto end with Rocket escaping with the Mew-derived channel? |

## Branch 2: Johto

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Core idea | Johto is about Rocket turning folklore into a machine. Rocket is not summoning Celebi by force at first. They are using Unown patterns, radio infrastructure, Apricorn capture craft, and old Rocket signal networks to predict when Celebi will appear to repair time damage. |
| Starting version | The adult Johto hero returns to New Bark Town. Elm gives the player a harmless local errand. Ethan, Kris, Silver, or another Johto ally becomes the local partner. The first Rocket incident is a Sprout Tower raid. At first, the raid looks like a theft of old records, ritual notes, or growth research. |
| First visible crime | Sprout Tower is raided during what should have been a local Violet errand. Rocket steals old tower records, ritual timing notes, and unusual growth observations. Elm, Silver, and the player did not know Rocket was active before this. |
| Investigation shape | Johto should slowly become stranger. Plants recover too quickly, bells repeat a rhythm no one rang, people remember moments that have not happened, and Unown patterns suggest Rocket is predicting a repair event instead of calling Celebi directly. |
| Emotional center | Celebi should feel sacred rather than mechanical. The player should understand that Celebi appears to heal harm, not to serve people. Rocket's crime is that they create harm on purpose so a guardian has no choice but to appear. |
| Current ending target | Celebi appears to repair the damage Rocket caused. Rocket springs a prepared trap and captures Celebi. The alliance learns that Johto supplied the temporal channel. |
| Questions to build | What is the Johto hero's adult life before the story begins? What ordinary errand pulls the player toward Violet? What does Rocket steal from Sprout Tower? How early should the time anomalies begin? Which Johto locations are essential for this branch? How does Johto end with Rocket capturing Celebi? |

## Branch 3: Hoenn

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Core idea | Hoenn is about Rocket turning wishes into extractable energy by combining modern industry, stolen power infrastructure, ancient Hoenn seals, meteor science, and Jirachi folklore. The story begins as a Devon industrial theft, expands when Rocket steals New Mauville's old generators and ships them through Slateport, then turns ancient when the trail points toward the Sealed Chamber and the Regi sites. Rocket is not chasing the Regis as trophies: Regirock helps locate meteor and wish-energy signatures, Regice helps disturb Jirachi's protected thousand-year sleep, and Registeel gives Rocket the flexible containment breakthrough needed to hold Jirachi during wish surges. The Regi readings eventually reveal that Jirachi sleeps beneath Sootopolis in an ancient meteor cradle, but Rocket still needs Mossdeep's comet-tracking science to know when that sleep becomes vulnerable. Hoenn should feel like a region where old myths, buried machines, sea routes, space science, and a hidden wish beneath Sootopolis all point toward the same dangerous machine. |
| Starting point | The documented Hoenn branch starts when the player reaches Rustboro City. Everything before this can stay in the game, but this file begins with the first main-story trigger: Team Rocket running out of Devon Corp. |
| Rustboro trigger | The player arrives in Rustboro and sees Rocket grunts fleeing Devon Corp with a sealed Devon case. A Devon employee calls for help, but the scene is chaotic and no one understands what Rocket wanted. |
| First player action | The player battles one Rocket grunt near Devon Corp or on the way out of the city. The player wins, but the Rocket members still escape with the stolen item. This gives the player a small win while making it clear the robbery succeeded. |
| Rocket escape | Rocket gets away, and the player is not entirely sure where they ran off to. There is no clean chase lead yet, only the feeling that something important was stolen and the trail has gone cold. |
| Rival arrives | The rival arrives after the battle because they had already told the player they were heading to Rustboro to challenge the Gym. They ask what happened, see that Rocket has escaped, and realize this is bigger than a normal city disturbance. |
| Birch lab suggestion | The rival decides to return to Birch's lab to talk through what happened and suggests that the player should do the same. This points the story back to Birch without making the player or rival official heroes yet. |
| Rustboro aftermath | Devon employees and city officials secure the area after Rocket escapes. Roxanne is kept separate from the initial incident so the player can still challenge the Rustboro Gym naturally before or after returning to Littleroot. Rustboro treats the event as a major crime, not yet a mythic crisis. |
| Optional Devon context | If the player stays and talks to Devon employees, they explain that Rocket stole an energy-focusing core. The employees do not ask the player to recover it. At this point, the item is simply lost, and nobody is treating the player or rival like chosen heroes. |
| Devon's first explanation | Devon describes the stolen core as technology meant to focus unusual energy. The important clue is not Jirachi or wishes yet. The player only knows Rocket stole a serious Devon prototype whose purpose is still unclear. |
| Littleroot report | The player and rival return to Birch's lab and explain that Team Rocket robbed Devon Corp. Birch becomes visibly concerned because Rocket should not be operating openly in Hoenn, especially not against a major research company. He does not turn the stolen Devon item into the player's recovery mission; that would be Devon's responsibility. Instead, Birch treats the robbery as a warning sign and says he will contact Oak, Elm, Devon, and trusted Hoenn researchers without alarming the public. Birch asks the player to continue their Hoenn journey while watching for broader signs of Rocket activity, especially around research sites, energy systems, weather stations, meteor studies, and unusual Pokemon behavior. The player is gathering observations Birch can compare with other reports, not being sent to catch Rocket or retrieve Devon property. The rival also wants to help, so Birch sends them on a different support task. |
| Split assignment | Birch asks both the player and rival to meet trusted contacts and ask whether they have seen signs of Team Rocket operating in Hoenn. The male character is sent to Mauville to speak with Wattson. The female character is sent to Slateport to speak with Captain Stern. This means the player personally sees a different side of the investigation depending on the gender selected at the start, while the rival handles the other city. |
| Mauville contact | Wattson is retired as Gym Leader, but he remains the person who understands Mauville's power systems and old infrastructure best. Flannery now runs the Mauville Gym, while Wattson plays the technical expert role. His task is not to identify the Devon item; it is to say whether Rocket has been asking questions about New Mauville's retired generators, old maintenance tunnels, heavy power equipment, or anything that could move large amounts of energy away from Mauville. |
| Slateport contact | Captain Stern is the Slateport contact because he understands shipping, research cargo, shipyard equipment, ferry routes, and technical supply chains. His task is not to track Devon property; it is to say whether suspicious people, false company names, strange cargo requests, or unusual route questions suggest Rocket has been quietly moving through Hoenn before the Devon robbery. |
| Slateport task | Stern asks the character assigned to Slateport to ask around the harbor and check shipping documents for anything unusual. They find records of heavy industrial cargo being booked under false company names, including equipment too large for ordinary shipyard repairs. The route is disguised as local freight, but the paperwork is deliberately vague. |
| Mauville task | Wattson asks the character assigned to Mauville to check old maintenance logs and speak with people who still remember New Mauville's shutdown. They learn that strangers have been asking about retired generators, service lifts, and which pieces of equipment could still run if removed from the station. |
| Contact report | After completing the local task, the player reports back to Stern or Wattson. In the middle of that conversation, the rival calls the player on the PokeNav and asks them to meet in Verdanturf. |
| Verdanturf meetup | The player meets the rival in Verdanturf and also meets Wally. The rival explains what they discovered in the other city, then mentions that they ran into Wally during their investigation. |
| Wally introduction | Wally is now a strong Pokemon Trainer known by the locals, not a sickly or weak beginner. He becomes part of the Hoenn storyline here because he knows the region well, has local trust, and can help connect the investigation to ordinary people affected by Rocket's activity. |
| Wally's suggestion | Wally suggests that the player, rival, and Wally should all go talk to Wattson together. The scene then jumps to the group speaking with Wattson in Mauville. |
| Wattson's conclusion | After hearing both the Slateport and Mauville reports, Wattson decides the strange activity is serious enough to investigate New Mauville directly. He believes Rocket is not trying to steal power from the grid; they are trying to steal the old machines that once made New Mauville dangerous. He gives the player Surf so the player and rival can reach and investigate the station. |
| New Mauville investigation | The player and rival investigate New Mauville while Wattson and Wally continue checking Mauville itself. Inside New Mauville, they discover Team Rocket removing old generators from the station. Most of the generators are already gone, but the player and rival stop a few Rocket members before they can take the last one or two machines. |
| New Mauville aftermath | Wattson confirms that the stolen generators are not valuable as ordinary power supplies. They are old, unstable, and difficult to operate safely, but they can produce intense bursts of energy without drawing from a public grid. This makes them useful for a hidden machine that Rocket wants to run somewhere far from Hoenn's power systems. |
| Slateport generator trail | The investigation returns to Slateport because the stolen New Mauville generators are too large to move quietly by road for long. Stern and the player find that several heavy crates already passed through the harbor. The destination is concealed behind false route transfers, so the player does not yet learn that the generators are being shipped to Cinnabar. |
| Slateport second clue | While checking the shipping trail, the player and rival also hear that Rocket has been asking about currents, dive routes, old stone chambers, and a difficult-to-reach stretch of sea beyond Pacifidlog. This does not clearly connect to the generator theft yet, but it suggests Rocket has a second operation near Slateport's sea routes. |
| Sealed Chamber seed | The player does not yet know the name Sealed Chamber, but Rocket's questions are later revealed to point toward the underwater ruin that opens the way to the Regi sites. For now, the clue should feel like a loose end: Rocket stole generators through Slateport, and something else is happening somewhere out on the currents. |

## Shared Sevii Arc

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Core idea | Sevii opens after Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn are resolved. It should feel like the alliance finally seeing the full machine, not like another isolated regional errand. |
| Main allies | Celio, Lorelei, Bill, island elders, and a possible Rocket informant help expose Rocket's black-site chain. Their role is to connect shipping routes, encrypted traffic, island folklore, and Birth Island coordinates. |
| Rocket's island network | Rocket uses the islands for warehouses, relay stations, decoy docks, and hidden research away from mainland League oversight. The islands should explain how Rocket moved materials from every region without being stopped earlier. |
| Deoxys target | Birth Island is the Deoxys climax. Rocket interrupts or exploits the Deoxys encounter with adaptive containment tech designed for a Pokemon that can mutate its own form. |
| Current ending target | Rocket captures Deoxys and ships it to Cinnabar. The alliance learns that Sevii supplied the mutation channel, completing Giovanni's machine. |

## Shared Finale

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| First Cinnabar assault begins | The heroes assault occupied Cinnabar. The player enters the Rocket citadel beneath the island, moving through the old Pokemon Mansion, lab ruins, and lower Rocket facility. |
| Giovanni reveals the machine | Giovanni reveals that the Mew-derived channel was already embedded in Mewtwo. The Mythic Siphon empowers Mewtwo using the channels stolen from the regional branches. |
| Giovanni vanishes | Giovanni tries to command Mewtwo with the completed machine. Mewtwo rejects him and makes him vanish in a psychic flash, leaving his fate uncertain. |
| Forced loss | The player battles empowered Mewtwo and always loses. The loss should feel like proof that no normal team can defeat Mewtwo in this state. |
| Celebi's intervention | Celebi breaks free after the defeat and sends the player back in time before the assault fully collapses. This turns the finale into a second-chance structure instead of a simple boss rematch. |
| Faraway Island | The player arrives in the past with knowledge of what will happen at Cinnabar. The player must go to Faraway Island to find Mew, because Mew is the only Pokemon capable of battling Mewtwo directly. Faraway Island should feel quiet, ancient, and separate from Rocket's machinery. |
| Mew chooses to help | Mew does not become a simple captured tool. It chooses to help. The player returns to Cinnabar with Mew instead of catching and using Mew like a normal party Pokemon. |
| Second Cinnabar assault begins | The same major events begin to happen again. Giovanni activates the Mythic Siphon, Mewtwo rejects him, and Giovanni vanishes again. The player now has the knowledge and ally needed to change what happens next. |
| Oak vanishes | Oak protects the team from Mewtwo's psychic wave and vanishes. The second assault still has a personal cost, even though the player has returned with Mew. |
| Mew battles Mewtwo | This time, the player and Mew arrive before the story reaches the same failed ending. Mew battles Mewtwo directly, holding it long enough for the player and allies to reach the captured mythics. |
| Mythics are freed | During the Mew and Mewtwo battle, the player and allies free the captured mythics. Celebi escapes immediately. Deoxys escapes immediately. The player or another major character manages to bond with Jirachi. |
| Wish Tag payoff | Jirachi's Wish Tags are used to remove Mewtwo's stolen mythic power. This pays off Hoenn's wish theme and makes Mewtwo vulnerable without turning the player into the one who simply defeats it by force. |
| Main story ending | Once the stolen power is removed, Mew can overpower Mewtwo. Mewtwo escapes after being freed from the extra power, and Mew also escapes. The main story ends with Rocket defeated, the mythics free, Oak missing, Giovanni's fate uncertain, and Mewtwo unresolved. |

## Open Questions

| Phase | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Hoenn Regi connection | How does Rocket use the Sealed Chamber and the Regi sites to locate, awaken, or contain Jirachi without making the Regi trio feel like unrelated checklist legendaries? |
| Hoenn Jirachi setup | Who should bond with Jirachi, and how should Wish Tags be introduced early enough that the finale payoff feels earned? |
| Kanto emotional beat | What scene best explains the cost of the original Mewtwo project and makes the Kanto branch feel morally important? |
| Johto anomaly level | How visible should time anomalies be before Celebi appears? Should they start subtle and eerie, or become obvious sooner? |
| Sevii reveal | Which island should reveal Deoxys as the final target, and how long should the player think Sevii is only about Rocket logistics? |
| Finale replay | How much of the first failed Cinnabar assault should repeat during the second assault before the player changes the outcome? |
